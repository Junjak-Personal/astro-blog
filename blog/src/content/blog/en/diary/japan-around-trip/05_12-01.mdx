## Journal  
  
### Ine no Funaya  
  
Today, I woke up around 8 o'clock, got ready, and left the dorm around 9 o'clock.  
It was a lovely change to have the owner see me off and wish me a good day of sightseeing.  
  
I got on the bus and got off at the Ineman Meguri cruise ship stop instead of the Ine stop, which is right near Ine-no-Funaya, and walked while looking at the surrounding scenery.  
The weather was a bit cloudy, which was disappointing, but it felt good to walk through the quiet fishing village. It’s a different feeling from the Tomonoura I went to last time.  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105225848219.webp" alt="image-20250105225848219" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105225900314.webp" alt="image-20250105225900314" />  
  
A path led toward a shrine along the way, so I stopped in. Perhaps it was a branch of Yasaka Shrine?  
It felt untouched by people. Was it maintained only once every few years?  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105230012889.webp" alt="image-20250105230012889" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105225957123.webp" alt="image-20250105225957123" />  
  
I could also see a cruise ship sailing from afar.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105230205373.webp" alt="image-20250105230205373" />  
  
I saw Mukai Brewery on the way, so I stopped by.  
Unfortunately, there wasn't a tasting option. Looking through the bottles for sale, I spotted Ine Mankai, the sake I'd enjoyed with oden yesterday, and bought one.  
Some of the higher-end sake, such as junmai daiginjo, caught my eye too. But I couldn't keep adding to my luggage, so I passed for now.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105230251284.webp" alt="image-20250105230251284" />  
  
As the photo shows, Ine Mankai cost 1,210 yen without a box.  
It was a bit surprising that the description said it was an ancient rosé. Just as I felt...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105230353633.webp" alt="image-20250105230353633" />  
  
It took about 20 minutes on foot to arrive at the main street of Ine-no-Funaya, where the tourist information center is located.  
I picked up a few leaflets at the tourist information center and bought a mascot pin badge as a souvenir. (I didn't take photos inside...)  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105230614693.webp" alt="image-20250105230614693" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105230732679.webp" alt="image-20250105230732679" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105230747759.webp" alt="image-20250105230747759" />  
  
I spent some time looking at the scenery of Ine-no-Funaya, taking pictures, and then moved towards my next destination, a restaurant where I would have lunch.  
Along the way, I found a museum-like place where you could look inside a funaya boathouse, so I stopped in. Admission was 200 yen.  
There were various fishing gear and other exhibits, and there were also texts explaining the history of Funaya, etc.  
Looking out from inside a boathouse at the rows of other boathouses was enjoyable too. Worth stopping by at least once.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105231146977.webp" alt="image-20250105231146977" />

On the way there, I took a picture of a seagull standing proudly on the roof.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105231256177.webp" alt="image-20250105231256177" />  
  
Around 11 a.m., I arrived at Ine Teuchi Soba Higashikaze for lunch.  
I was there almost at opening time. There were no other customers yet, and the owner was still getting ready, so I went inside around 11:05.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105232328328.webp" alt="image-20250105232328328" />  
  
The lunch menu was soba, and the dinner menu at the izakaya had sashimi, etc.  
I asked the owner if I could also order from the izakaya menu, and he said that I could order except for a few menu items.  
I ordered hot tsukimi soba and the chef's choice of two kinds of sushi: sagoshi, young Spanish mackerel, and aji, horse mackerel.  
Since I was having fish with it, I chose a chilled junmai muroka nama genshu from Yosamusume Brewery as my sake pairing.  
For some reason, the sake was listed on the menu simply as "Hana."  
It had a nice balance of sweetness and umami. At 17% ABV, the alcohol was a little assertive too. Not bad.  
The sashimi was well aged, fresh, and delicious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105231807369.webp" alt="image-20250105231807369" />  
  
After I finished the sashimi and sake, the hot tsukimi soba arrived. The soy sauce-based broth was nicely seasoned, and the handmade noodles were good. The portion of soba wasn't particularly large.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105232249011.webp" alt="image-20250105232249011" />  
  
I still felt like having a little more after the soba. A customer at the next table ordered sashimi and, on the owner's recommendation, chose warasa, young yellowtail.  
Ine is known for yellowtail shabu-shabu, and I was curious about the local fish, so I ordered the same thing. I paired it with Horyo Junmai Karakuchi Muso, served warm.  
The customer next to me had already ordered two go of the same warm sake. He kindly let me have one go of it, so I could start drinking sooner.  
Despite the name Karakuchi Muso, it didn't taste especially dry. It had a moderate amount of rougher, miscellaneous flavors.  
The warasa was fatty and delicious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105232419630.webp" alt="image-20250105232419630" />  
  
  
A total of 4,700 yen after eating like this.  
To give a general review of the store, both the sashimi and soba were delicious, but since the owner ran the store alone, the menu was quite slow to come out.  
If you visit, I recommend giving yourself plenty of time. And please note that it only operates on weekends.  
  
After lunch, I walked toward Funaya no Sato Park. There were quite a few stairs, a good way to work off the meal.  
I'd recommend visiting for the view over Ine's boathouses from above.  
I had a matcha soft-serve ice cream from the park shop for dessert.

### Amanohashidate  
  
After finishing the tour of Ine-no-Funaya, I wanted to see the daytime scenery of Amanohashidate, so I took a bus and moved towards Kono Shrine.  
The entrance to Amanohashidate from the Kono Shrine side looks like this.  
When I went there yesterday, I was able to see things (small shrines, trees, sculptures, etc.) that I couldn't see properly because it was night.

Crossed Amanohashidate and headed to Chionji.  
The temple enshrines Manjushri, the bodhisattva associated with wisdom, so people apparently pray here for academic success...  
It seems like a good temple to stop by at least once while sightseeing in Amanohashidate.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105233953214.webp" alt="image-20250105233953214" />  
  
I paid 10 yen and lit some incense too.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105234605851.webp" alt="image-20250105234605851" />

There were unusual fan-shaped omikuji hanging from a pine tree, so of course I tried one. Medium luck this time.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105234128069.webp" alt="image-20250105234128069" />  
  
Leaving Chionji behind, I headed to Amanohashidate Viewland, the second main destination of the day.  
Similar to Kasamatsu Park, you need to travel by cable car or lift chair.  The price is 800 yen round trip.  
I’ve already ridden the cable car, so this time I’ll take the lift chair.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105234823636.webp" alt="image-20250105234823636" />  
  
When I got to the top, the view of Amanohashidate that I had seen in the photo caught my eye.  
It was disappointing that the curved shape of the scenery at Kasamatsu Park was not visible, but it was nice to see the curved shape of Amanohashidate clearly at Viewland.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105235326697.webp" alt="image-20250105235326697" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105235009997.webp" alt="image-20250105235009997" />

<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105235116548.webp" alt="image-20250105235116548" />  
  
There were several attractions inside, but they were all for children, so if adults were to visit here, there wouldn't be much to do other than look at the scenery.  
Still, the scenery alone seems to make it worth paying 800 yen to come here.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105235239787.webp" alt="image-20250105235239787" />  
  
I'd arranged a 3,000-yen sashimi set through the guesthouse for dinner. It was scheduled for 6:30 p.m., leaving me plenty of time,  
I decided to stop by a nearby cafe.  
I visited the stylish-looking Jouzou Coffee and had an oat milk latte.  
The price is 750 yen. Compared to the price, the taste was just so-so.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250106000859164.webp" alt="image-20250106000859164" />  
  
After spending about 30 minutes at the cafe, I headed across Amanohashidate to my accommodation.  
On the way, I also saw the bridge rotating to allow a cruise ship to pass.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250106001234395.webp" alt="image-20250106001234395" />  
  
On the way to the accommodation, I stopped at a supermarket and bought cup ramen, small cans of beer, and a few snacks.  
When I returned to the accommodation, the owner prepared sashimi, and the photo below is the sashimi.  
It was a lavish spread for the price: six slices of yellowtail back, four of yellowtail belly, eight of salmon, four shrimp, six of Spanish mackerel, and ten of squid.  
(Note, the sashimi is purchased from a store the owner knows and provided.)  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250106001641643.webp" alt="image-20250106001641643" />  
  
I started with a tiny Asahi Hitokuchi beer. I wish they sold these little one-sip cans in Korea too...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250106001919720.webp" alt="image-20250106001919720" />  
  
The pictures below are what I ate for dinner.  
A combination of cup ramen, cheese, and senbei bought at the supermarket (a Japanese snack combination I heard on the radio.), sashimi set.  
As for cup ramen, since I came to Kyoto, I ate Kyoto Shoyu ramen, and it was moderately delicious. I was a bit surprised to see that it also included chashu and menma.  
The sake pairing was Haten, which I'd bought at Morita Brewery in Kurashiki.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250106002033847.webp" alt="image-20250106002033847" />  
  
Five years of aging had given it rich umami. Despite being 17–18% ABV, it barely had any alcoholic bite, and its moderate sweetness was lovely.  
The label said "slightly dry," but to me it seemed closer to sweet than dry.  
I tried warming it too. Warming sake usually brings out more umami, but this one didn't seem to change much, which was a little disappointing.  
There wasn't much difference between warm and chilled. Still, it was delicious to begin with, so I was happy.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250106002314176.webp" alt="image-20250106002314176" />  
  
I offered the owner a taste, and he said it was good. (He'd said he wasn't keen on sake, but this one seemed to suit him?)  
I was drinking alone, and the owner and his wife were having a barbecue in the yard, so they kindly gave me some pieces of charcoal-grilled meat.  
As expected, charcoal grilling, a taste that doesn’t betray you.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250106002352370.webp" alt="image-20250106002352370" />  
  
The owners of this guesthouse are friendly and the prices are reasonable, so I recommend it if you ever visit Amanohashidate.
