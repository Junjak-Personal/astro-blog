My first stop was Shinise Memorial Hall in Nagamachi.  
The entrance fee was cheap at 100 yen.  
The store, which used to be a pharmacy, was reorganized into a museum and was on display.  
  
It recreates Nakaya Pharmacy, a business founded in 1579 that operated through the Meiji period.  
The building itself dates from the end of the Edo period and served as both pharmacy and home. It was moved to Nagamachi in 1987... Inside were displays of medicinal herbs, the shop, and the family's living quarters.  
It was fascinating to see an old established Japanese pharmacy, something you rarely get to experience.

After looking around the fermentation exhibition hall, I entered the inside of the museum.  
There are two types of admission tickets: a 400 yen ticket that allows admission to some of the permanent and special exhibitions, and a slightly more expensive ticket that allows admission to all exhibition halls.  
There are areas that can be viewed for free, so there is no need to purchase a separate ticket. (Admission itself is free.)  
Modern art isn't a major interest of mine, so I chose the cheaper 400-yen ticket. The sightseeing bus pass gave a 40-yen discount, bringing it to 360 yen.

Higashi Chaya District is lined with teahouses and shops. My first stop was Fukumitsuya.  
It's apparently a well-known Kanazawa brewery. A sake brewery? Of course I had to go in.  
The Japanese sake made here is a brand called Kagatobi (加賀鳶).  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250109020748893.webp" alt="image-20250109020748893" />  
  
Various types of Japanese sake were sold inside, and tasting was also possible here.  
There were quite a few types of alcohol available for tasting, and I ordered the seasonal Jizake tasting set. The price is 1750 yen.  
The types are Ginjo Shinshu, Junmai Daiginjo Nigorizake, and Hatsuage Junmai Namagenshu.  
  
For the first time, I drank Junmai Daiginjo Nigorizake. Perhaps because it is Junmai Daiginjo, it was very clean, so it felt a little different from the Nigorizake I had eaten before.  
Next is Ginjo Shinshu.  I think the clean taste stood out as it was freshly squeezed sake.  
Lastly, Hatsuage Junmai. As the rice polishing rate was 70%, it had a bit of an unpleasant taste and the alcohol taste was a bit strong.  
All three types were reasonably delicious, but I didn't feel it was anything special.

Lastly, while thinking about where to go, I stumbled upon Sanneitei (茶寧庭), a stylish shop.  
There weren't many reviews, but it had a rating of 5 on Google Maps, so I visited.  
I was greeted by a newly-built interior with a fresh scent and a smartly dressed bartender.  
Although it was small with a total of 5 seats, it was a very sophisticated cocktail bar, and the bartender was quite young. He looked to be around 30 years old.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250109023527283.webp" alt="image-20250109023527283" />  
  
Looking at the menu, I saw that there was a sake-based cocktail, so I ordered one after receiving a recommendation from the master.  
My first drink was Permanent Box, apparently one of the bar's popular orders.  
It combined local Ishikawa sake, egg white, lemon, and Hendrick's gin, topped with miso, shiso, soy sauce, and sake-lees powders.  
  
Overwhelming visuals welcomed me.  
The taste is creamy and refreshing thanks to the egg whites.  
The visuals were great, but the taste was also very good.  
The powders were arranged on the corners of the box, letting me taste different combinations with the drink.  
The soy sauce and miso powders added saltiness and their individual aromas, creating new flavors.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250109023829004.webp" alt="image-20250109023829004" />  
  
The second glass is Sake & Jasmine Cocktail.  
The ingredients were local Ishikawa sake, Yoshidagura u Ishikawamon, jasmine, lemon, white port, and st-germain.  
  
The bartender said its sweetness made it popular with women. The jasmine aroma highlighted the sake's sweetness, making a delicious cocktail.  
There was also a muscat scent, perhaps due to the influence of white port wine and lemon.  
He served something like yokan sweet bean jelly as a snack.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250109023907682.webp" alt="image-20250109023907682" />  
  
The third drink was Matcha Jewelry, customized with shochu.  
The original used an ingredient called Passiond. When I asked for a sake- or shochu-based cocktail, he adapted it with shochu.  
Matcha, Passiond, coconut water, and the addition of sweet potato shochu.  
  
Kanazawa gold dust as a garnish for a green drink. The visuals were significant.  
He said the alcohol content was in the high teens, but the strong matcha aroma made it feel more like 5%.  
It felt like drinking a wonderfully tasty green tea drink.  
With the third drink, he served another wagashi, a sweet filled with red bean paste.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250109024000179.webp" alt="image-20250109024000179" />  
  
The fourth cup is a sake coffee cocktail.  
The ingredients are coffee, Ishikawa Prefecture Jizake (Tengumai Yamahai Shikomi Junmaishu), and white port wine.  
  
At first glance, it seems like a combination that would not go well at all, but I felt that the port wine played a role in mixing the two well, and the Japanese sake had a coffee aroma but a sweet taste. It was very delicious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250109024328530.webp" alt="image-20250109024328530" />  
  
The fifth glass is Green Harmony.  
The ingredients were Hendrick's gin, yuzu soda, lime, local Ishikawa sake—Tengumai Yamahai Shikomi Junmai—and kiwi.  
  
The kiwi scent dominates, and as yuzu cider is added, the citron scent is also present.  
Gin and sake act as alcohol, but they don't feel like alcohol. It had a strange taste, but it was delicious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250109024859886.webp" alt="image-20250109024859886" />  
  
The 6th glass is Japanese sake and Yamawasabi.  
The ingredients are Ishikawa Prefecture Jizake (Tetorigawa Junmai Daiginjo), wasabi, pear, and the rest I didn't take a proper photo of so I don't know...  
  
It smells and tastes like pear, and the wasabi comes in later, which is a kick.  
The junmai daiginjo sake kept it clean-tasting while supplying plenty of alcohol.  
Of everything I drank here, this was my favorite.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250109024932700.webp" alt="image-20250109024932700" />  
  
The 7th drink is Naybrief & Curry Cocktail The Kanazawa.  
The lineup of ingredients was spectacular: pear, lemon, lime, mango, curry powder, egg white, coconut water, etc...  
  
A cocktail with the sweetness of mango enveloping you, while the bitter and spicy taste of curry hits you hard.  
Trying a new flavor was fun. Personally, though, it was my least favorite of the drinks I had here.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250109025311289.webp" alt="image-20250109025311289" />  
  
The 8th glass to finish is Nihonshu & Lemon Matoru.  
Ingredients: Lemon, Bombay Sapphire, Jasmine Tea, Ishikawa Prefecture Jizake (Yoshida Gurayu - Ishikawamon)  
  
A carbonated, high-quality lemonade-like drink with a sour finish after enjoying various flavors.  
It felt like a very suitable cocktail to finish with.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250109025525977.webp" alt="image-20250109025525977" />  
  
Total of 11,400 yen including charge, 8 cocktails, and wagashi.  
The price was less than I expected, and I thought it was not a waste of money to have such a rare experience for only 100,000 won.  
  
The bartender was meticulous and uncompromising in the best sense, using Ishikawa-produced ingredients wherever possible and even making powders and cocktail ingredients himself. I felt the bar thoroughly deserved its five-star Google rating.  
The customer service was excellent, the master was very friendly, and he was willing to prepare arranged cocktails that were not on the menu according to the guests' tastes. The master had a high level of skill.  
  
There were plenty of other cocktails, but sake cocktails are hard to find elsewhere. I focused on those rather than the other signature drinks.  
I have 100% intention to return and will try other types of cocktails when I visit again.  
This cocktail bar made today the best day ever.

I was a bit drunk, so I took a messy photo of the store.

I was a bit tired after drinking alcohol, so I wanted to go to the hot springs, so I decided to go to a super sento called Manten-no-yu near my accommodation.  
This is a super sento that many locals visit and is not much different from the super sentos that exist in many places in Japan.  
  
Bathing fee is 850 yen for non-members, bedrock bath is 500 yen. Towels are not provided separately, so you have to purchase them for 250 yen (purchase, not rental).  
Fortunately, the staff explained that signing up for membership—even as a foreign visitor—would give me a 200-yen discount on the stone sauna. Including the towel purchase, I enjoyed everything for 1,400 yen.  
The baths consisted of 4 indoor baths (cold bath, massage bath, foot bath, hot bath), 3 outdoor baths (herbal bath, carbonated bath, lying bath), and 2 types of sauna (wet bath, dry bath).  
The stone sauna area was quite small: one room at around 50 degrees, and another in the high thirties with ocean sounds playing.  
There were few types of comic books.

After bathing, I was hungry, probably because I'd skipped lunch. I decided to visit Ramen Kabukimono, the place I'd been too full to try yesterday.  
The store was quiet, and there was one office worker who came to eat, two men who were cheering up the mood by talking about women around them, and the owner.  
  
I ordered assorted chashu, shoyu ramen, and a Kirin beer, for a total of 2,420 yen.  
The shoyu ramen had a clean flavor and just the right saltiness. Quite good.  
Chashu Moriawase is composed of pork and chicken and is salty, making it a perfect snack for beer.  
It didn't feel that delicious compared to the Google rating of 4.5, but it was pretty good.
