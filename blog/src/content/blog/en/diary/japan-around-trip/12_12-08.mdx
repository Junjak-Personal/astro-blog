## Journal  
  
Today's schedule is Takayama. From Nagoya Station, I took the limited express Hida train departing at 9:39 and went to Takayama.  
I sat in a window seat, D, and the scenery along the way was lovely.  
I highly recommend a D seat... Along the way, I saw a rainbow, mountains with snow only on their summits, and a green river near Gero.  
When I searched the weather for Takayama/Shirakawa-go on the Internet, it said that snow usually falls in mid-December.  
I was a little worried that I might not be able to see the snowy scenery, but the clear sky in Nagoya gradually became cloudy as I got closer to Takayama, and then it started snowing, so I didn't have to worry about that.  
After riding the train for about 2 and a half hours, I arrived in Takayama.

It really is Hyouka's setting. I later realized that a stamp rally was also underway.  
I noticed it when I left Takayama City, so unfortunately I couldn't participate.

The ryokan itself felt like a museum. The building had an old feel and many old exhibits were on display.  
Of course, the management was neat and well done.

I arrived at the accommodation earlier than the check-in time and originally planned to just leave my luggage.  
They said I could check in, so I checked in right away and unpacked my luggage.

I left the accommodation and went straight to lunch.  
The lunch menu was Chinese soba, so I visited the Yayoi soba main store right in front of the accommodation.  
https://maps.app.goo.gl/Ky7jhi1gyZQCHJN6A  
  
Compared to the reputation of the store, the Google Maps rating was quite low at 3.5 points at the time, and the reason was the attitude of the staff serving customers.  
While I was discussing what to eat with my companions, he suddenly approached me and asked me to be quiet...  
It wasn't something that was said very loudly.  
After getting the menu from the ramen ticket vending machine, I sat down and didn't say much, but I was warned again to be quiet.  
I thought it was anti-Korean, but when the Japanese men next to me finished eating and left,  
It wasn't noisy, but I think the staff member was just a strange person since he asked them to be quiet.  
  
If you look at the Google Map reviews, the taste was good, but most of them gave low ratings due to the staff's attitude towards customers.  
The ramen itself was actually delicious.  
I ordered chashu shoyu ramen. The pork was lightly charred on the outside, crisp at the edges and moist inside. The broth was clean and good too.  
The extra rice balls I ordered were quite poor for 250 yen, so I was a bit disappointed.  
A total of 1,500 yen came out.

After lunch, I decided to walk down Takayama Street.  
The snowfall was getting stronger, and the snowy streets of Takayama had a nice atmosphere and it was enjoyable to walk.

As I was walking, I saw a large torii gate. I was curious to see what kind of shrine there was, so I went closer and it turned out to be Sakurayama Hachimangu Shrine.  
The main deity is Hachiman, famous as the god of war and military affairs.  
It's an old shrine, said to date to around 377, during Emperor Nintoku's reign.  
It was the central shrine of the Takayama Matsuri on October 9th and 10th, and there were festival-related buildings and museums inside.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113052731952.webp" alt="image-20250113052731952" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113052916295.webp" alt="image-20250113052916295" />  
  
I came to the shrine. Omikuji. 100 yen per time.  
There were several types, one of which was Omikuji, which operates by inserting a coin like a vending machine.  
If you put money in, the shrine maiden doll in the photo will randomly pick up an omikuji from the shrine and take it out.  
It was my first time seeing this structure, so I was quite surprised.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113052942573.webp" alt="image-20250113052942573" />  
  
Next is Mizumikuji, 300 yen per session.  
The feeling was similar to the Mizumikuji I picked at Kanazawa Shrine last time.  
Surprisingly, I got great luck.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113053415542.webp" alt="image-20250113053415542" />  
  
I've traveled a lot to Japan, but this is my first time coming to a shrine while it's snowing.  
The snowy scenery of the shrine was quite new, and it was nice to be in that space itself.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113053547494.webp" alt="image-20250113053547494" />

The place I visited was nearby Tabinoshiori https://maps.app.goo.gl/XeZKfqBYfuU4Aq2f7 , which had a good rating.  
It was a cafe that was also operated as a guesthouse.  
The feel of the interior was also quite good.  
The combination of the woody interior, appropriate lighting, and the snowy scenery outside the window stimulated an indescribable emotion.  
The BGM was Ghibli-style too, perfectly matching the atmosphere. It felt like a real-life version of those lo-fi cafes you often see on YouTube.

<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113055334452.webp" alt="image-20250113055334452" />

The difference is that there are more retro games. However, since you had to pay separately to play it, there wasn't much of an advantage.

<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113060015642.webp" alt="image-20250113060015642" />  
  
I originally planned to visit Harada Shuzo next door, but it closed early, so I ended up only visiting Funasaka Shuzo.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113055829515.webp" alt="image-20250113055829515" />  
  
The characteristic of Funasaka Shuzo is that it has a well-equipped tasting room for tourists. There were quite a few Koreans as well.  
Tasting is a system in which medals are exchanged at a medal vending machine and then tasting is performed using each tasting machine.  
Drinking glasses had to be purchased separately, but could be exchanged for one medal.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113060033458.webp" alt="image-20250113060033458" />  
  
Most Japanese sake could be tasted with one medal, but some Junmai Daiginjo grade could be tasted with two medals.  
With more than 20 tasting dispensers, there was plenty of variety. You could even try the sake hot.  
However, most of the taste of the liquor was dry and not to my taste, so I think I only tasted about 7 to 8 glasses.  
Among them, the most delicious ones were Shiboritate Namazake Junmai Ginjo Miyamagiku Muroka Namagenshu.  
It was tasting number 21 with a purple label... It was a little dry, but the carbonation of the nama was good.  
I didn't take any pictures...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113060103240.webp" alt="image-20250113060103240" />  
  
I didn't buy alcohol separately, but I saw Shirakawa-go cider on sale as a souvenir, so I bought one.  
The label has a cute design with the Hida mascot and Shirakawa-go's Gassho-zukuri building.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113061230902.webp" alt="image-20250113061230902" />  
  
After the tasting, before dinner, we needed to prepare for tomorrow's visit to Shirakawa-go,  
I went to Takayama Bus Terminal to pick up tickets in advance.  
When I arrived at the bus terminal, local character Takumi Takayama greeted me.  
  
I looked it up and found out that it was called the Onsen Musume Project, which was part of a project to promote and promote by creating characters for each famous hot spring area, and Takayama was also included in this project.  
There are over 100 characters, so it seemed like it would be fun to collect goods while visiting each hot spring.  
https://onsen-musume.jp/character/#unit  
  
Anyway, I bought a round-trip bus ticket to Shirakawa-go, but the price was a bit expensive at 5,600 yen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113061502847.webp" alt="image-20250113061502847" />  
  
After purchasing a round-trip ticket to Shirakawa-go, I went to Hidagyu Maruaki for dinner.  
We arrived around 5:30 and there were 16 teams waiting in front of our team.  
Three of those 16 parties didn't return in time, and tables turned over faster than expected, so we got in after about half an hour.  
As I was waiting, I felt that more than half of the people were Chinese.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113062338684.webp" alt="image-20250113062338684" />  
  
The menu I ordered was A5 Hida beef assortment, Hida beef sukiyaki, and Hida beef Akami sukiyaki. A total of about 15,000 yen, including rice, etc.  
For 3 people, it's about 5,000 yen per person.  
It was a shame that I had to grill the meat myself.  
The taste of the meat itself was fatty and delicious. A little softer than Korean beef?  
But there doesn't seem to be a big difference.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113062744341.webp" alt="image-20250113062744341" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113062848327.webp" alt="image-20250113062848327" />  
  
Honestly, I enjoyed the Hida beef sukiyaki more than the A5 Hida beef assortment.  
So I ordered another sukiyaki.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113062819583.webp" alt="image-20250113062819583" />  
  
Hidagyu Maruaki felt closer to a tourist attraction than a restaurant.  
Is it because there were so many Chinese tourists who felt like they had to stop by and try the food if they came to Takayama?  
Compared to the reputation, the service was a bit disappointing.  
If you want to eat Hida beef in Takayama City, I think it would be better to go somewhere quieter and more visited by locals instead of here.  
  
  
  
After dinner, I wanted a hot spring bath. We headed to Takayama Green Hotel, which the tourist information center had recommended for day-use bathing when we first arrived.  
The Green Hotel is a fairly large hot spring hotel with a Hida beef restaurant and a large souvenir shop.  
There were maple trees that had not yet fallen, so I was able to see the rare combination of snow-covered maple leaves.  
The interior was clean and pleasant too. I almost regretted not booking a stay here.  
  
The cost was 1,500 yen for bathing fee and 500 yen for bath towel, for a total of 2,000 yen.  
Of course, I couldn't take pictures inside because photography was prohibited, but it was a large public bathhouse with two internal baths, one sauna, and two open-air baths.  
The open-air bath was well-designed with a nice atmosphere, and it even snowed.  
One of my wishes was fulfilled: enjoying an open-air bath on a snowy day.  
The hot spring water was okay as it was a simple, slightly alkaline hot spring and had a slippery feel.  
The facilities were good and the open-air bath was great, so it became a hotel I wanted to stay at in the winter.  
If you like hot springs, I think this is a must-visit place, so I highly recommend it.

After enjoying the hot springs, I decided to go to a bar to end the day.  
I chose Bar Thumbs up, whose business card I'd seen at Sumiyoshi Ryokan.  
The location was right in front of Sumiyoshi Ryokan, so it was convenient to walk to in 20 seconds.  
 https://maps.app.goo.gl/sNcn4E8XL3FN4iibA  
  
I was lucky because I entered an hour before closing and there were no customers.  
A bar that sells various types of alcohol, but beer is the main focus. (No cocktails)  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113064543142.webp" alt="image-20250113064543142" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113064855957.webp" alt="image-20250113064855957" />  
  
The menu I ordered was 2 types of beer and snacks - smoked quail eggs, fried potatoes, and fried tofu.  
I had a craft IPA—ordinary enough that I don't remember which one—and Duchesse de Bourgogne.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113064837248.webp" alt="image-20250113064837248" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113064648018.webp" alt="image-20250113064648018" />  
  
Duchesse de Bourgogne was so good that I took a separate photo of its menu entry.  
It is said to be a blend of beer aged for 18 months and beer aged for 8 months in oak barrels. The oak aroma, ale-like fruity taste, and acidity give it a beer that I really like.  
I thought about purchasing it separately, but gave up because I was worried that traveling would be difficult if I had too much luggage.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250113065159824.webp" alt="image-20250113065159824" />  
  
The owner kindly answered questions with an appropriate sense of distance and informed me of various things, so I was very satisfied.  
I got the feeling that it didn't have a Google rating of 5 for no reason.  
My share came to around 3,000 yen: two beers and a split of the snacks.  
  
We left about five minutes before closing, returned to the ryokan, wrote up the day's travels, and went to bed.
