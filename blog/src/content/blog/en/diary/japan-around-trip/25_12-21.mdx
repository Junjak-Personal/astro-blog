### Asakusa  
  
Since my accommodation was near Asakusa, I briefly looked around at the Sensoji scenery in the morning on the way to Akihabara.  
I'd considered Monjayaki Tetchan near the hotel because it had appeared in The Solitary Gourmet with Goro-san, but unfortunately the timing and route didn't fit my fixed itinerary.  
  
For breakfast, I ordered a kimchi gyudon set meal from nearby Sukiya.  
The total price was 940 yen, which was a bit expensive for a gyudon franchise, but I thought prices had risen a lot in Japan as well.

### Kanda Shrine  
  
After a quick morning walk, I moved to Akihabara.  
Before going to Akihabara Otaku Street, I decided to visit Kanda Shrine to digest the meal I ate first.  
  
There was a small, unidentifiable horse at the entrance to the back gate, so I took a picture of it because it was fascinating.  
I looked it up and found out that the mascot of Kanda Myojin is called Shinmaro Akari-chan.  
It was a bit surprising as it was my first time seeing such a small horse.  
  
The shrine's office had a lot of goods, omikuji, etc. on display, giving it a very secular feel.  
I couldn't take pictures inside because there was a sign prohibiting photography.  
I drew a 200 yen enmikuji here once, but forgot the results because I didn't write them down.

### Akihabara  
  
After visiting Kanda Shrine, I walked to Akibahara.  
The street is full of things to hate from the moment you enter.  
The difference from the previous time I went was that there were more mobile gacha games on the main page than animations/comics.  
  
I arrived before 10 o'clock and many stores were not yet open.  
While walking, I found the famous Radio Kaikan, and there were a lot of otakus waiting to buy goods.  
To pass the time until the shops opened, I went into the Atre building and stopped at Starbucks. Atre was running a collaboration with Is the Order a Rabbit?, but unfortunately Starbucks wasn't participating...  
  
After killing time at a cafe a little after 10 o'clock, I started exploring Akiba in earnest.  
I bought some Bocchi the Rock and Freelen keyrings and spent about 2,000 yen.  
Unusually, there was also a building related to Warhammer, so I went into it.

### Nakameguro  
  
After seeing Akibahara, I went to Nakameguro Station because I was planning to go to Starbucks Reserve.  
It was a system where you had to wait to enter through on-site QR, and there were quite a lot of people waiting in line.  
I decided to kill time at the park by waiting to get in.  
There was a mistake in this process, so the waiting time inevitably became longer, and I ended up waiting for about 2 hours.  
  
Nakameguro itself is a famous wealthy area in Tokyo, so I thought it would be a good place for a walk as the streets are clean and quiet.  
There are two parks near Reserve Roastery: Sugekari Park and Saigouyama Park.  
Sugekari Park was a park that warmed my heart as I saw quite a few families with their children coming to play.  
Reviews of Saigoyama Park said that you could see Mt. Fuji, but I couldn't see it.

### Starbucks Reserve Roastery

The impressive interior features a huge roastery machine and tubes transporting coffee beans.  
They sell not only coffee but also bread, so I bought a strawberry pie and a croissant. I ordered a barrel aged cold brew, and my friend ordered a latte.  
I looked around the inside of the store until the menu was ready.

### Starbucks Reserve — Terrace & Menu  
  
Despite the chill, we sat on the outdoor terrace. There were hardly any seats available inside.  
I ordered the coffee because I really like drinks that have been aged in oak barrels, but as it was aged in oak barrels, it had a woody and caramel flavor.  
However, the flavor was not deep and light, probably because it had not been aged for a long time, which was a bit disappointing to me.  
  
There was also a coffee cocktail bar on the third floor.  
They were selling various menus, but they all cost more than 30,000 won, so I gave up because I was running out of time and the prices were high.  
  
Personally, I think the taste of the coffee was disappointing compared to the price, but the impressive interior, the luxurious and sophisticated feel you can experience at Starbucks, and the ambience created by the coffee bean transport pipe, roastery machine, and props provided here and there seem to be worth the 2-hour wait and the price, so I think it is worth coming to.

### Azabudai Hills  
  
The next destination is Azabudai Hills.  
It is famous for a redevelopment project that took more than 30 years, and I heard that it finally opened a year ago, so I was interested in going there, and I took this opportunity.  
The entire street had a new feel and a sophisticated and luxurious atmosphere.  
It's also a good point to have a clear view of Tokyo's symbol, Tokyo Tower.  
  
Because it was close to Christmas, things like illuminations and winter event stores were being held, so it was crowded with people.  
The streets were lined with brands far beyond my budget, such as Hermes and Prada, and the shops inside JP Tower were generally very expensive too.  
There were a lot of interesting props, so it seemed like it was worth window shopping.

### Kani Jigoku  
  
After seeing Azabu, I moved to Shimbashi for dinner.  
Since I had some time left until my reserved visit to Kani Jigoku, I decided to kill some time before entering.  
  
What I ordered was king crab, kani ryōshinabe, kani miso, kamameshi, and nomihodai.  
In Korea, crabs are usually eaten steamed and difficult to prepare, so although they are delicious, I was reluctant to eat them, but since they were grilled and prepared to a certain extent, they were easy to eat and delicious.  
Personally, I found it more delicious to eat it grilled than to eat it steamed.  
  
This was my first visit since coming here two years ago. Back then it felt like a local place frequented only by Japanese customers, but now it seemed to draw plenty of tourists too.  
There is also a menu for foreigners... I feel a little sad about it being turned into a tourist spot.
