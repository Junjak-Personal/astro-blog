## Travel Diary  
  
### To the Northern Culture Museum  
  
  
  
#### breakfast  
  
To get to the Northern Culture Museum, take a bus from Niigata Station.  
There wasn't a special pass available, so I had to pay the bus fare in full both ways.  
This day was not a very good day with rain falling on and off again.  
  
First of all, I looked around Niigata Station in the morning to have breakfast, but there were only a few restaurants open.  
Among them, I found Yanagian, a tachinomi udon/soba restaurant for train passengers, and entered.  
Orders are placed at the kiosk in front, and prices were very affordable, around 400–600 yen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301033201143.webp" alt="image-20250301033201143" />  
  
What I ordered was Tentama Udon with Tenkasu topping, for a total of 640 yen.  
What's surprising is that the food came out less than a minute after ordering.  
Looking at the kitchen, it was fast because they prepared all the ingredients in advance and added udon, broth, and toppings as ordered.  
The taste was average, but the quality of the udon was quite good considering the price.  
There was no feeling of any loss of freshness at all.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301033458013.webp" alt="image-20250301033458013" />  
  
Additionally, Niigata is famous for its rice, so I bought mentai onigiri at the rice ball shop in the station, but I didn't notice much difference from other onigiri.  
  
#### Northern Culture Museum  
  
After breakfast, I took the bus and got off at a stop near the Northern Culture Museum. It took about 45 minutes from Niigata Station and the fare was a bit expensive at 880 yen.  
The area near the bus stop was deserted and sparsely populated, and the waiting area seemed quite old, perhaps because it had been built a long time ago.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301033936745.webp" alt="image-20250301033936745" />  
  
If you walk 5 to 6 minutes from the stop, you can see the entrance to the Northern Culture Museum.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301034238836.webp" alt="image-20250301034238836" />  
  
It was raining quite heavily when I arrived, so my feet were all wet.  
<VideoLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/20241215_013016000_iOS.MOV" />  
  
Enter by paying an admission fee of 800 yen.  
Speaking of the Northern Culture Museum, at first I thought it was a place that collected the culture of Niigata, which is close to the northern part of the Tohoku region.  
The origin of the name is not specific, and it is said that it was named after an open-air museum in Stockholm, Scotland.  
The Northern Culture Museum occupies the former estate of the Ito family, major landowners in Echigo. Its roughly 8,800-tsubo grounds are enormous, and the garden is impressive.  
It didn't look much different from the mansions I'd visited so far, but since it was a large landowner, the scale was completely different.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301034622798.webp" alt="image-20250301034622798" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301034635260.webp" alt="image-20250301034635260" />  
  
Daihiroma is the first thing you see when you search for the Northern Culture Museum.  
I could see a well-maintained garden from a large space.  
The scenery was so wonderful that it could be said to be the best among all the Japanese-style mansions I have ever visited.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301035549714.webp" alt="image-20250301035549714" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301035559274.webp" alt="image-20250301035559274" />  
  
The direction of Daihiroma seen from the second floor window.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301035715513.webp" alt="image-20250301035715513" />  
  
At the museum on the second floor, you could see the chronological records of past Ito clan heads.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301035618920.webp" alt="image-20250301035618920" />  
  
This is the exhibition hall and lake outside the main residence.  
I wanted to look around more because the site is large and there are a lot of things to see, but since the buses only run every 1 to 2 hours,  
If you miss this bus, you will have to wait over 2 hours for the next bus.  
I had no choice but to look around for about an hour and a half and then take the bus back to Niigata Station.

### Pia Bandai  
  
#### Sanja Shrine  
  
After returning to Niigata, I took a bus towards Pia Bandai, and there was a shrine nearby, so I went there.  
The name is Sanja Shrine. It is said that the name originated from the fact that it worships three gods.  
The size of the shrine is not that large, so I think it would be a good place to stop by before heading to Piabandai.  
It is famous as a shrine related to the transportation industry...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301041115911.webp" alt="image-20250301041115911" />  
  
I took a picture of the frog omikuji because it was impressive.  
The omikuji cost 300 yen. I drew one and got chukichi, moderate good fortune.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301041034828.webp" alt="image-20250301041034828" />  
  
#### Piabandai Market  
  
Next, enter the Pia Bandai market.  
As an indoor fish market, I didn't feel anything special about it.  
I looked around briefly and came out.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301041301322.webp" alt="image-20250301041301322" />  
  
Outside, they were selling grilled fish, grilled scallops, etc.  
I was thinking about trying one, but gave up since I was planning on eating a lot of conveyor belt sushi for lunch at a restaurant.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301041349319.webp" alt="image-20250301041349319" />  
  
Next, I went into the nearby Pikari Market, which was an indoor market with shops selling other items besides fish.  
Perhaps because the New Year was approaching, I noticed Matsukazari being sold.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301041528209.webp" alt="image-20250301041528209" />  
  
#### Lunch: Conveyor belt sushi Benkei  
  
After looking around the market for a bit, I went to Benkei Pia Bandai to buy conveyor belt sushi for lunch.  
As expected, because it is a famous restaurant, the wait was quite long.  
There were 17 teams in front of me, and the turnover rate was faster than expected, so I was able to enter after waiting for about 30 minutes.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301041625876.webp" alt="image-20250301041625876" />  
  
This is what the inside of the store looks like.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301041823578.webp" alt="image-20250301041823578" />  
  
The picture is a bit blurry, but I ate a total of 11 plates and a beer, for a total of 5,731 yen.  
One of the things I ate was grilled butter hotate.  
It tasted all right, but I wouldn't particularly recommend it.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301041955931.webp" alt="image-20250301041955931" />  
  
Ootoro and Nodoguro.  
Nodoguro is a premium fish and was quite expensive, but it wasn't to my taste.  
The Ootoro had a lot of fat and was soft and delicious.  
It was the most expensive plate (770 yen), but it was disappointing that there was only 1 piece...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301042046139.webp" alt="image-20250301042046139" />  
  
It was a Zuwai warship, and the crab taste was average.  
I was a little disappointed that the fish was cold. Still, it was delicious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301042141563.webp" alt="image-20250301042141563" />  
  
As it is famous, there is a wait, but there are not many tourist attractions in Niigata city, so it is highly likely that you will go to Pia Bandai.  
If you come to Pia Bandai, I think it would be recommended to come here for lunch.  
I was satisfied because the quality of the fish was good compared to the price and the shari was delicious.  
  
After lunch, I looked around some other nearby stores and then moved on to my next destination.

### Imayo Tsukasa Brewery  
  
#### Visit a nearby shrine  
  
After having lunch at Pia Bandai, I decided to take a break and walk to my next destination, Imayo Tsukasa Brewery.  
While I was walking, there was a shrine nearby so I stopped by.  
The photo below shows Nuttari Kotohara Shrine, a small shrine with only a torii gate at the entrance and the main shrine.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301042616947.webp" alt="image-20250301042616947" />  
  
This is Nuttari Hakusan Shrine.  
Although it is slightly larger than Kotohara Shrine, it is still a small shrine.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250301042605312.webp" alt="image-20250301042605312" />  
  
#### brewery  
  
After a brief tour of the shrine, I arrived at my destination, Imayo Tsukasa Brewery.  
There were many foreign tourists here. (Although most of them are Chinese...)  
A special feature of this brewery is that tours of the brewery are also available.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250302224727425.webp" alt="image-20250302224727425" />  
  
At the entrance to the brewery, there were various products related to alcohol brewing.  
It had the most diverse range of products out of all the breweries I've visited so far, including Baumkuchen made with Japanese sake in wooden barrels and ice cream made with sake kasu.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303003827960.webp" alt="image-20250303003827960" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303004140574.webp" alt="image-20250303004140574" />  
  
At the entrance, there was a plan in place called Sake Kasu Tsume Hodai, where you could take sake Kasu with you as soon as you put it in a plastic bag.  
I carried about 3 kg on average and the maximum recorded was 9 kg...  
Later, the staff member giving the brewery tour said that people were taking too much money and were losing money.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303003946946.webp" alt="image-20250303003946946" />  
  
There is a QR code for brewery tour application at the counter, and tour reservations can be made through QR.  
It was possible to make a same-day reservation for a Japanese-language tour, but the English-language tour was only open once a week, so there seemed to be some competition.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303004219972.webp" alt="image-20250303004219972" />  
  
Brewery tour starts at 3 p.m.  
It was my first time actually going inside the brewery, and it was cool inside, perhaps because they were storing rice and alcohol. Around 10 degrees?  
In the photo below, rice for brewing was stored, and you can already see plain rice and rice that has been polished.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303004355407.webp" alt="image-20250303004355407" />  
  
After passing this door.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303004610709.webp" alt="image-20250303004610709" />  
  
You can see the tanks storing alcohol as follows, and it is impressive that they have different capacities.  
The reason was that it was difficult to make it precisely due to the large capacity of the tank, but looking at the age of the tank, I thought it was worth it.  
These green tanks were made during the Showa era.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303004641438.webp" alt="image-20250303004641438" />  
  
The next warehouse I visited was relatively recent, dating back to Heisei 5.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303004845674.webp" alt="image-20250303004845674" />  
  
Pipes and hoses could be seen throughout the brewery, through which alcohol from Moromi was transported.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303004947275.webp" alt="image-20250303004947275" />  
  
The only areas open to visitors were the liquor storage room and the filtering area that could be seen through the window.  
Still, it was amazing because it was my first time actually seeing a brewery.  
The photos below show several exhibits showing the history of the Imayo Tsukasa brewery on the last course of the tour.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303005105340.webp" alt="image-20250303005105340" />  
  
After the brewery tour, I went on to sample the sake being made at Imayo Tsukasa.  
There are two types of tasting: free tasting and paid tasting. There are three types of free tasting, as shown in the photo below.  
The two types on the left tasted good, with a slightly firm peach and sake with a green grape flavor.  
The sake on the right was a dry style, so it wasn't quite to my taste.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303005404620.webp" alt="image-20250303005404620" />  
  
Paid tasting can be done by going to the counter and paying 1,000 yen. The 12 types of sake you can taste are shown in the photo below.  
If you pay at the counter, you will receive a plastic cup, which feels a bit more luxurious than the free tasting cup.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303005550843.webp" alt="image-20250303005550843" />  
  
I tried all the paid liquors, but most of them were from the Tanrei Karaguchi series.  
These weren't the rich, sweet styles I prefer, so I realized that this brewery wasn't really to my taste after all.  
Still, there is one thing I liked: the 2,000 yen Amaguchi junmai ginjo, which is on the cheap side.  
The English description said super sweet, but it wasn't quite that sweet: just moderately sweet and refreshing. With its low alcohol content, it seemed approachable even for people who aren't comfortable drinking, as the description suggested.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303010142505.webp" alt="image-20250303010142505" />  
  
The above liquor was more delicious than ‘Koi’, which has been promoted for generations as a signature drink.  
The bottle design was pretty nice and seemed suitable as a gift.  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303010422212.webp" alt="image-20250303010422212" />  
  
It was impressive to see 'Koi' placed in a nearby fish tank along with carp.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303010509685.webp" alt="image-20250303010509685" />  
  
This is what the entire tasting room looks like  
The sake wasn't quite my style, so I only paid for a tasting and tried the sake-lees pudding; I didn't buy any bottles.

### Guesthouse Cuckoo  
  
The weather was poor, with rain starting and stopping, so I decided to rest at the guesthouse and perhaps visit a nearby super sento later. For now, I took the bus back to my accommodation.  
In the guesthouse lounge, the staff was remodeling the interior, so I helped them a little and warmed up in the kotatsu.  
As I shared a few stories from my travels and mentioned that I liked omikuji, they brought this out and suggested I give it a try.  
It is a constellation omikuji, and if you put 100 yen into your constellation and spin it, an omikuji will appear.  
I had never seen this kind before, so I drew one as a souvenir. The result was chukichi, moderate good fortune.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303011327156.webp" alt="image-20250303011327156" />  
  
#### Gokurakuyu  
  
When I mentioned that I was going to the super sento Gokurakuyu in Sendai next, one of the staff, Kouta-san, said he would come too. I unexpectedly ended up getting a ride there with him.  
I talked about various things on the way, and he said he likes Korean movies and sake.  
Among the movies I watched, I felt like I saw Parasite, Train to Busan, etc.  
I don't watch many films, so it felt as though he knew more about them than I did.  
In addition, as I talked about the history of Korea and Japan, the military, etc., I arrived at Gokurakuyu.  
  
Looking inside, it was unusual to see a collaboration with Nishisanji.  
Other than collaboration, the structure is not much different from super sentos in major cities in Japan.  
Admission fee is 800 yen. The towel rental fee was a bit expensive at 400 yen, so I went in with the towel I received from the hotel.  
The composition of the baths is adequate, with 4 indoor baths, 3 open-air baths, and 2 saunas. There were quite a few people too.  
After taking a bath, Kota-san had a hard time driving, so I bought him some milk as a reward for his hard work.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303011847963.webp" alt="image-20250303011847963" />  
  
#### Dinner: Guesthouse Cuckoo  
  
After warming up my body with a bath, I had dinner at the guesthouse.  
What I ordered was Kani Vienna and today's pasta. They were 300 yen and 1,200 yen respectively.  
Kani Vienna is a Vienna sausage made into a crab shape, and tastes like a normal Vienna sausage.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303012358463.webp" alt="image-20250303012358463" />  
  
Today's pasta was mentai pasta, and it was a little salty for my taste.  
The taste of the food was just okay.  
If you don't feel like going out and want to eat while talking to the staff in the lounge, it's not a bad idea to try it once.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303012411528.webp" alt="image-20250303012411528" />  
  
#### Sake tasting party  
  
On the way back from the bathhouse after dinner, I talked to Kota and had a sake tasting event.  
I'm going to try the kotasan and the sake I have, comparing the taste.  
It was a pity that I couldn't take a good picture.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303024618634.webp" alt="image-20250303024618634" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303024626099.webp" alt="image-20250303024626099" />  
  
The first thing I drank was Ringo Cider Sake purchased in Nakano City. The bottle with the CIDER label located first in the first photo.  
It was disappointing that the sweetness and acidity expected from apple liquor was a bit light, and the alcohol taste was strong.  
  
The second drink I drank was Kokuryu Kijoshu. It is a small bottle with a red label located 3rd in the first photo.  
I've tried it once before, but it tastes delicious even if I try it again.  
It was sweeter than regular sake and had the unique flavor of Kijo-shu, making it delicious.  
  
From then on, I drank with other staff and guests in the lounge.  
  
The third one is Sanrensei Gentei unfiltered Namagenshu Junmai Shikagumi, which has purple visible on the label in the second photo.  
There was an unidentifiable bitter taste at the end of the amami, but it felt okay in its own way.  
The carbonation that could be felt from namagenshu was also good.  
  
The fourth thing I tried was Mifuku Honjozo, located third from the left in the second photo.  
On Kota-san's recommendation, I tried it warm. Heating it brought out the rustic rice notes and umami characteristic of a less highly polished honjozo, which I quite liked.  
  
Next, Daishinshu Hito Gokochi GI Nagano purchased in Nakano. It wasn't photographed.  
The taste was clean, sweet, and had umami.  
I felt like it was an appropriate accompaniment to the meal, and it was also Daishinshu. it's good.  
  
Lastly, my favorite Masuizumi Barrel Aged Kijoshu.  
Since this drink is very sweet, I recommended eating it with dessert or as a finishing touch, and everyone who attended the tasting agreed after trying it.  
Sweet taste followed by oak and caramel aromas.  It's amazing that sake can come out with such a taste.  
  
While drinking sake with Japanese people, I evaluated each other's taste...  
In the middle, I talked about animations I both enjoyed, and I had similar tastes, such as Bocchi the Rock and Free Ren, so I was able to talk at length.  
It was a really fun time as I have rarely experienced anything like this while traveling.
