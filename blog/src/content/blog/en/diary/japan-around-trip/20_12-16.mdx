## Travel Diary  
  
### To Akita Prefecture  
  
I left the familiar Furumachi shopping street in Niigata and headed to Niigata Station to go to Akita Prefecture.  
On the way to catch the bus, there was an unidentifiable animation and manga university.  
It just looks like a professional academy...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303170620225.webp" alt="image-20250303170620225" />  
  
#### morning  
  
I liked the Yanagian restaurant I had yesterday for a light breakfast at a reasonable price, so I visited again.  
When I ate it yesterday, the seaweed was a bit bothersome, so I ordered one tanuki udon without the seaweed for 490 yen.  
It is also good for a light meal.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303170109165.webp" alt="image-20250303170109165" />  
  
After eating breakfast, I arrived at the platform to take the train.  
There is no Shinkansen to the next destination, Ugo Honjo Station, so I took the limited express Inaho.  
Departure at 8:22, arrival at 11:24, cost 6,580 yen.  
It is a fairly long journey.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303165853092.webp" alt="image-20250303165853092" />  
  
On the way, I organized a bit of yesterday's travel journal and put labels on the alcohol I drank in my notebook.  
It's a shame that the label was slightly damaged...

### Yurihonjo poetry feat. Yuki no Bosha  
  
After traveling for 3 hours, I arrived at Ugohonjo Station in Yurihonjo City.  
The station itself was quite large, but there were hardly any people there.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172823580.webp" alt="image-20250303172823580" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303171324960.webp" alt="image-20250303171324960" />

In the waiting room, special products from Yurihonjo City were displayed.  
What caught my eye there was today's purpose, Yuki no Bosha's sakes.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303171416902.webp" alt="image-20250303171416902" />  
  
There was also a separate space where Gotenmari, one of the specialties, was displayed.

#### Yuki no Bosha  
  
I took a bus and walked from the station to the Yuki no Bosha brewery, but I took the wrong bus and the 30-minute trip took almost an hour.  
  
Yuki no Bosha arrived like that. It felt like the brewery itself was off-limits to anyone other than officials.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172255998.webp" alt="image-20250303172255998" />  
  
I was wondering if there was a shop where I could buy alcohol, but when I saw a review, I found that there was a place called Hakkoukouji Taya across from the brewery.  
You can buy alcohol...  
Here, there was a shop selling Yuki no Bosha-related products and a cafe that appeared to be run by Yuki no Bosha.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303171737271.webp" alt="image-20250303171737271" />

I purchased it 2 years ago, but the label has changed.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303171946649.webp" alt="image-20250303171946649" />

Here I bought chosetsu, snacks, goods, etc.

#### Hakko Koji Taya Cafe  
  
I decided to have lunch at Taya Cafe to commemorate my visit to Yuki no Bosha.  
The menu I ordered was the Osakana Plate Drink Set on the menu below.  
For drink, I ordered black vinegar cider.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172547300.webp" alt="image-20250303172547300" />  
  
The food came out with the same composition as in the photo.  
Salad, grilled salmon, hamburger, boiled eggs, rice balls and pickled vegetables.  
The rice balls were made with sakekasu rice and had a unique taste and texture.  
The salmon was well-cooked and soft, and the hamburger was especially flavorful and delicious.  
Black vinegar cider feels like adding carbonated water to high-quality Schisandra chinensis tea. Anyway, it was delicious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172504475.webp" alt="image-20250303172504475" />  
  
For dessert, I ordered a soft drink made with daiginjo sakekasu and Baumkuchen. The price is 600 yen.  
The soft-serve had the taste and aroma of amazake and paired nicely with the baumkuchen. I enjoyed it.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172532451.webp" alt="image-20250303172532451" />  
  
After having lunch like that, I looked around the space behind the cafe, and there was a place called Yuki no Bosha club.  
It seemed like various experiential activities were held here, but they were not applicable today.  
I took a quick look inside and came out.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172601949.webp" alt="image-20250303172601949" />  
  
#### Honjo Park and Yurihonjo City  
  
After lunch, I went for a walk to Honjo Park.  
Since this place is not a famous tourist destination, there are few people and only cars are visible.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172617563.webp" alt="image-20250303172617563" />  
  
I arrived at Honjo Park on foot, and it is said that Honjo Castle used to be located there.  
Similar to other castles in Japan, it was demolished by an order to close the castle after the Meiji Restoration.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172626784.webp" alt="image-20250303172626784" />  
  
The park grounds were quite large, but mostly empty, with little in particular to see.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172641860.webp" alt="image-20250303172641860" />  
  
At the location where the castle tower was previously located, there was a museum called Susu-gwan where the castle's relics were displayed, but today was a closed day, so I was not able to enter and only took pictures from outside.  
It seemed to have been completed fairly recently, as the building still felt new.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172702351.webp" alt="image-20250303172702351" />  
  
There were also small shrines inside.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172717725.webp" alt="image-20250303172717725" />  
  
It was interesting that there was no separate memorial box, so the memorial fee was placed on a wooden frame.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250304031342526.webp" alt="image-20250304031342526" />  
  
After taking a walk around the park, I walked back to Ugohonjo Station because it was almost time for the train to Akita City.  
Yurihonjo City Cultural Exchange Center I found on the way.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172747517.webp" alt="image-20250303172747517" />  
  
I took a picture of the building because it was a bit unusual, and it was quite a huge building for a small city.  
There were quite a few people inside as if there was an event going on.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172759233.webp" alt="image-20250303172759233" />  
  
Walk back to the waiting room at Ugo Honjo Station.  
There were about 40 minutes left until the train arrived, so I decided to look at the pamphlets in the waiting room.  
The thing that caught my eye the most was the bear caution poster.  
Because it is a place with a lot of mountainous terrain, they say that you should never enter the no-entry areas, as there may be bears there.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172849996.webp" alt="image-20250303172849996" />  
  
Next, I looked at some tourist pamphlets, including introductions to special products, shrines, waterfalls, etc.  
There were quite a few interesting things.  
Of course, it's hard to get there without a car because transportation isn't that good, so I probably won't be able to go in my life...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172918784.webp" alt="image-20250303172918784" />  
  
When the train time was up and I went to the boarding area, the train was slow.  
Since there aren't many passengers, it seems like there's nothing I can do?  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172931776.webp" alt="image-20250303172931776" />  
  
### In Akita city...  
  
About 40 minutes from Ugohonjo Station? Take the train and arrive at Akita Station.  
Akita Station was quite large, probably because it was the center of the prefecture, and was crowded with people, including students and office workers.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173041772.webp" alt="image-20250303173041772" />  
  
The station was decorated with a variety of famous items from Akita, giving it a different feel from other subway stations I've been to before.  
Among them, the Akita dog and Namahage were impressive.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303172950189.webp" alt="image-20250303172950189" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173056857.webp" alt="image-20250303173056857" />  
  
First, take the bus to the accommodation and check in.  
The location of the accommodation was on the Minami Odori side, and I was satisfied because there were many restaurants and izakayas nearby.  
  
After checking in, the tempered glass film on my phone was broken, so I decided to go to Don Quijote to get it replaced.  
It had been snowing on and off since Yurihonjo City, but when I got to Akita City, the snowfall was quite rough.

#### Mega Don Quixote  
  
Approximately 15 minutes walk from the accommodation. I arrived at Don Quixote by walking a short distance.  
Here at Don Quixote, no one looked like a tourist, and local people seemed to visit like they were going to Homeplus or E-Mart.  
It was quite large in size, and there were other chain stores inside, so it was a space where you could do everything from shopping, dining, and cultural activities.  
  
I purchased a tempered glass film that looked reasonably good, and looked around the inside.  
There were collaboration products, and a few Shipduck goods were also seen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173141931.webp" alt="image-20250303173141931" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173152840.webp" alt="image-20250303173152840" />  
  
It was impressive to see that some of them were collaborating with CU and even featured CU convenience store products.  
The prices were a little high for Don Quijote.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173205671.webp" alt="image-20250303173205671" />  
  
#### Dinner: Tokkuri noki  
  
I went back to the hotel, put away what I bought at Don Quixote, and went out to eat dinner.  
Tokkuri Noki was a store that caught my eye on the way to the accommodation. I decided to have dinner here tonight.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173415610.webp" alt="image-20250303173415610" />  
  
It wasn't a restaurant, but since I like visiting izakayas in various parts of Japan and eating local sake, I decided to have dinner and drinks.  
This menu is a selection of the alcohol sold here, and you can see that there are quite a few different types.  
What was disappointing was that the variety of local sake in Akita Prefecture was smaller than expected.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173219859.webp" alt="image-20250303173219859" />  
  
As I was choosing the menu, Otooshiro, boiled octopus, tofu, and pickled radish came out.  
The flavors were mild, but it seemed appropriate to whet the appetite.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173247837.webp" alt="image-20250303173247837" />  
  
 The first sake I ordered was Yuki no Bosha's junmai ginjo bishu no sekkei, which was made using 100% Yamada Nishiki from Hyogo Prefecture.  
The sweetness of the rice hidden in the clean feeling was wonderful.  
I found it surprising that even though it was a nama, there was no carbonation.  
As expected, Yuki no Bosha is delicious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173301869.webp" alt="image-20250303173301869" />  
  
While the food I ordered was being prepared, I ordered alcohol in advance, and the next sake was Harukasumi Junmai Yuki Label Nama.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173319831.webp" alt="image-20250303173319831" />  
  
The next menu item I ordered was stir-fried chicken with soy sauce.  
The meat was grilled like a steak, but the part was chicken leg, and the meat was well-cooked, so it was quite delicious.  
The pairing compatibility feels so-so.  
Because Harukasumi was a nama, it had a slightly carbonated taste and had a strong alcoholic feel.  
The Yuki no Bosha I had just drunk was so good that Harukasumi felt a little disappointing in comparison.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173331869.webp" alt="image-20250303173331869" />  
  
The chicken alone wasn't enough, so I ordered additional stir-fried shrimp with black soybean paste.  
It seemed a bit salty because it was miso, but the seasoning was just right, and I was satisfied with the chewy texture of the miso.  
However, it was disappointing that the amount of shrimp was a little small.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173344676.webp" alt="image-20250303173344676" />  
  
For pairing, Ippakusuisei's junmai ginjo.  
It was clean and suitable as a pairing, but Yuki no Bosha was the most delicious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173359961.webp" alt="image-20250303173359961" />  
  
In total, it came out to about 4,700 yen?  
A restaurant where both the price and taste of the food and drinks are satisfactory.  
I felt like this was a restaurant worthy of being called a good restaurant.  
  
#### 2nd sake container  
  
I was a little disappointed to end it with only the first round, so I decided to stop by the Oden restaurant and sake barrel that caught my eye on the way to Mega Don Quixote for the second round.  
From the outside, the store looks a bit shabby and looks like something local men would frequent, but the inside was luxurious.  
The clothes worn by the guests inside gave a formal feel.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173431217.webp" alt="image-20250303173431217" />  
  
I noticed dashi-wari on the menu, and the manager recommended it too, so that was what I chose.  
For reference, dashiwari is made by mixing sake or shochu with oden dashi broth.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173447598.webp" alt="image-20250303173447598" />  
  
When I ordered one Dashiwari, otoshiro snacks were provided as shown in the photo below.  
I was worried that the price might be a bit too high for an Otooshi.  
It consists of 5 types including tofu, sashimi, etc.  
The taste is not special, just okay to eat?  
I finished off a glass of Sake Dashiwari with Oshiman.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173459067.webp" alt="image-20250303173459067" />  
  
Next, I ordered oden, but this time it was Shochu Dashiwari.  
For oden, I ordered 5 types of Josho omakase.  
It consists of chikuwa, daikon, konjac, cabbage rolls, I can't remember the one...  
The oden taste is just an ordinary oden taste.  
The shochu dashi-wari tasted more strongly of alcohol and wasn't to my taste.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173507521.webp" alt="image-20250303173507521" />  
  
I wasn't that satisfied with both the alcohol and the food, and I was full so I decided to just eat this much and left the store.  
The amount paid was about 5,000 yen, which was cheaper than I expected.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250303173518004.webp" alt="image-20250303173518004" />  
  
They gave me a dessert pancake, and it tasted like eating old Korean snacks.  
For a pancake, it was quite thick.  
After eating my fill for the second round, I returned to my accommodation and finished the day.
