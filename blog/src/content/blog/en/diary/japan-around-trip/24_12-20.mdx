### Aomori Morning — Revisiting Utou Shrine  
  
Since I went to Utou Shrine late at night the day before, I couldn't even visit omikuji, so I decided to stop by before heading to Tokyo.  
On the way, I saw some Buddhist statues at an unknown temple wearing clothes for the winter, which I thought were cute, so I took a picture of them.  
It was still snowing heavily, just like yesterday.  
  
About a 10-minute walk from the accommodation, I arrived at Utou Shrine.  
It felt quite different from the evening scenery I saw the previous day.  
I was able to see the ema that I couldn't see properly yesterday because it was dark.  
Unusually, I saw Rilakkuma Ema. What is the relationship...  
  
I picked a Rilakkuma omikuji to commemorate it, but I don't remember the result.  
I saw a doll to ward off evil spirits near the memorial box, so I wrote it down and put it in.  
Toshikoshi Oharaishiki (年越大祓式) is a Japanese Shinto ritual that purifies oneself by transferring one's impurities to a doll and burning it or pouring it into a river during the Toshikoshi period...

### Nagao Chuka Soba  
  
For breakfast, I decided to visit Nagao Chuka Soba, the ramen shop recommended to me at Tenmai the day before.  
Niboshi ramen is a type of ramen that tastes like anchovy broth, and I was a little excited because I had never tried it before.  
There were quite a few celebrity autographs on display. A local recommendation seems to have paid off. Another bonus is the free rice.  
  
What I ordered was extreme niboshi ramen and torimomo chashu. They are 980 yen and 350 yen respectively.  
Just by looking at the color, it feels different from regular ramen. The soup seemed to be tonkotsu-based, but it had a strong taste of concentrated anchovy liquid.  
Out of curiosity, I ordered the extreme niboshi ramen, but I think the regular niboshi is appropriate for a ramen beginner like me.  
The taste was okay, but the seasoning was strong and the taste was so strong that it was a bit difficult to eat..?

### Asakusa Sensoji  
  
I joined my friend at Ueno Station and moved to Asakusa, our first destination.  
As of this trip, I've been to Tokyo 3 times and Asakusa 3 times so I'm familiar with it, but it's my friend's first time in Tokyo.  
For my first time, I put Asakusa Sensoji, an iconic tourist attraction in Tokyo, on my itinerary.  
  
My most recent visit was two years ago, and Asakusa Sensoji is still crowded with tourists.  
I wasn't very impressed because not much had changed.  
My friend was also famous, so I felt like I was looking around as if I had been there once.  
I also picked up an omikuji to commemorate it, was it a good luck? I don’t remember because it was an ordinary fortune telling.

### Hoppy Street & Asakusa Underground  
  
My friend and I hadn't had lunch yet, so we decided to grab a meal near Asakusa.  
The location is Sanchan Yokocho, on Hoppy Street, which is lined with many bars that caught my eye the last time I went to Asakusa.  
The Google rating wasn't that good, but I went in anyway.  
The menu I ordered was okonomiyaki, yakisoba, and two glasses of draft beer.  
The taste was nothing special, just ordinary okonomiyaki and yakisoba.  
It was a disappointing restaurant with poor customer service. I regret not having searched a little more before going in.  
  
After having a quick meal, I headed to Asakusa Station to go to Shibuya.  
I found a place in the basement with a retro feel and took a quick look around.  
I don't think I'd ever seen a place like this the last time I came, but I think traveling is fun because I discover new places every time I go.

### Shibuya Scramble  
  
While I've been to Tokyo twice, I've never been to Shibuya before, so I added it to my itinerary this time.  
I wanted to see the Shibuya Scramble intersection, so I tried to get off at Shibuya Station, but the road was quite complicated, so I ended up getting lost several times.  
You can see the Google building and there are many tall buildings, so it's hectic.  
  
Squid game-related promotions were also carried out on a fairly large scale.  
There were photo opportunities and small events, but neither my friend nor I was especially interested in Squid Game, so we just had a quick look and moved on.  
  
After wandering around a few times, I found the Shibuya scramble intersection.  
The huge billboards, crowds, and traffic made it incredibly busy and overwhelming.  
I took this photo from the Starbucks on the second and third floors of Shibuya Tsutaya. Down in the crossing, everything had felt hectic, but...  
The scenery seen from a distance seemed to show the colorful and busy city center well, so it was worth seeing.

### Shibuya Tsutaya  
  
There are several spaces in the Shibuya Tsutaya bookstore building other than Starbucks, so I took a look around.  
There was also a place that handled Pokemon cards, and there was also an advertisement for the recently popular Pokemon pocket.  
  
On another floor, there was a place to display and sell various animations and Virtuber goods.  
There was also merchandise related to Markain, which I enjoyed watching recently, and the V.W.P virtual music group, which I've been listening to quite a bit lately.  
Hololive's event was also being held on a large scale.  
I wanted to go into Mikorone Shrine, but unfortunately only those who made a reservation could enter, so I gave up.  
  
On the top floor, there was a cafe collaborating with Blank Pink’s Rosé.  
I thought about going in, but the price was around 2,800 yen... It was quite expensive, so I gave up.  
  
In the basement, an illustrator named Mika Picazo was holding an exhibition in collaboration with Disney.  
I really liked the artist's painting style, so I looked at the prices, and the cheapest one was around 1 million won...  
I decided to just save it in my head.

### Shinjuku — Hanazono Shrine & Golden Gai  
  
Next stop is Golden Gai in Shinjuku.  
When I went the year before last, I liked the atmosphere of the street, so I decided to go again.  
Following the same route as last time, I decided to stop by Hanazono Shrine and then go to Golden Gai.  
  
Hanazono Shrine is a shrine in the city near Shinjuku Station. Although it is located in busy Shinjuku, the grounds are quiet and a good place to take a short walk and rest.  
I took a photo of Inari Shrine, which is everywhere, rather than the main shrine, because it had more elegance with the fallen ginkgo leaves.  
  
After looking around the shrine for a while, enter Golden Gai. This place still has an impressive retro atmosphere.  
The store I decided to go to was BAR COO, which had a Google rating of 5.  
The store was quite small, and the customer who seemed to be a regular was having a birthday party or something, so the response wasn't very good for us, who were outsiders and foreigners.  
Last time I visited, both the owner and the customers seemed welcoming to outsiders. Maybe I was lucky.  
I paid a total of 2,100 yen for the seat + 2 drinks.  
  
With a feeling of regret, I entered a store called 5Gallons.  
It was a store where a hostess who spoke good English welcomed us.  
My friend could speak Japanese at a basic level, but his English was at a free-talking level, so he was conversing with the hostess in a mixture of English and Japanese.  
That look seemed quite refreshing.  
It wasn't as good as last time, but it was still a worthwhile experience.  
I paid a total of 2,600 yen for the seat fee and 2 drinks.
