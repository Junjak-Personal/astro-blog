## Journal  
  
### Kyoto City  
  
Because of the train schedule today, I had some time to spare, so I woke up at 8 o'clock, got ready, and checked out of the hotel at 10 o'clock.  
I traveled from Namba to Osaka Station, then on to Kyoto to catch the 12:25 Hashidate limited express for Amanohashidate.  
(On Google Maps, the jr train appeared to go directly from Kyoto to Amanohashidate...)  
  
I arrived at Kyoto Station around 11:10.  
There was still time before departure, so I decided to have lunch at Kyoto Station.  
I went to Soba Shubou Tokubei, a soba restaurant in the Kyoto Theater area, and ordered Kyoto curry udon and dashimaki rolled omelet. Both were ordinary enough. The bill was 1,925 yen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104053337462.webp" alt="image-20250104053337462" />  
  
I went to the ticket office at Kyoto Station to reserve a seat on the limited express, but my jr Rail Pass wouldn't cover the journey all the way to Amanohashidate.  
The station staff explained that the jr pass covered the trip only as far as Fukuchiyama. The section from Fukuchiyama to Amanohashidate is operated by the private Kyoto Tango Railway, so an extra fare was required.  
The Hashidate limited express itself runs all the way from Kyoto to Amanohashidate. At Fukuchiyama, the crew and operator change from JR to Kyoto Tango Railway. Looking only at Google Maps, I'd assumed JR covered the whole trip. Japanese railways are complicated...  
The staff said I could stay on the train and pay the additional fare to the crew later. It was 1,750 yen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104053403074.webp" alt="image-20250104053403074" />  
  
There was some kind of trouble, but the limited express Hashidate train was good.  
As it is a limited express, the train is pretty and the windows are large, making it easy to see the outside scenery.

### Amanohashidate  
  
After that little adventure, I arrived at Amanohashidate Station!  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105034837291.webp" alt="image-20250105034837291" />  
  
First, since I had luggage, I had to check in to the accommodation across from Amanohashidate Station.  
At first, I tried to walk, but my luggage was heavy, so I wandered around to see if there was any other means of transportation. I was told that I could go to the other side by motor boat, so I decided to take the motor boat.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105035325736.webp" alt="image-20250105035325736" />  
  
It was 600 yen one way or 1,000 yen return. I chose a one-way ticket, since I could walk back across later.  
Bicycles were also available as an option, but they could only be used for round trips and the price was 500 yen, so I decided on the faster and more comfortable motor boat.  
There were quite a few tourists on the Amanohashidate side, but it was a pity that motor boat business did not seem to be very popular.  
  
After arriving at the landing on the Kono Shrine side, I checked in to my accommodation first.  
It was a guesthouse called Furuminka Wa. Reservations are made by Instagram DM or phone.  
Amanohashidate is all hotels and ryokans, so I am thankful that they are running a guesthouse like this..  
https://kominkanagomi.crayonsite.info/  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105035533639.webp" alt="image-20250105035533639" />  
  
The room feels like this.  I thought that 5,500 yen for an entire room with TV was quite reasonable.  
A fridge and washing machine were available too. I used the washing machine that evening to get my laundry done.  
(As the owner lives in the house and operates two rooms as a guesthouse, you must inquire about using the washing machine.)  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105035858195.webp" alt="image-20250105035858195" />  
  
After checking in, I headed to the nearby Motoise Kono Shrine, which was quite large.  
On the way, I noticed some Darth Vader figures displayed outside a shop. What were these doing here?  
An exhibition featuring Darth Vader playing the guitar and Darth Vader eating a chicken leg... <ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105040347607.webp" alt="image-20250105040347607" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105040357068.webp" alt="image-20250105040357068" />  
  
The torii gate at the entrance of Motoise Kono Shrine looks like this  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105040430017.webp" alt="image-20250105040430017" />  
  
Before entering the torii at the entrance to the main shrine, wash your hands at Chozuya before entering.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105040526385.webp" alt="image-20250105040526385" />  
I've visited Ise Jingu before, and it felt a bit similar.  
Apparently, Amaterasu and Toyouke were once enshrined here before being moved to Ise Jingu, which is why Kono Shrine is also called Motoise.  
The area around Amanohashidate is called Tango, and this is considered its most important shrine.  
I got the impression that it was neatly and well managed.  
Photography is not allowed further inside here. I thought it was probably because it was the shrine that served as the origin of Ise Jingu.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105040617221.webp" alt="image-20250105040617221" />  
  
Omikuji if you come to a shrine!  
They had unusual umbrella-shaped omikuji, so I tried one. My fortune was suekichi, or future luck.  
I usually tie an uncertain fortune at the shrine and leave it behind, but this umbrella one was so distinctive that I kept it.  
I forgot to take pictures at the scene, so I took pictures after returning.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105041257757.webp" alt="image-20250105041257757" />  
  
After visiting the shrine, move to the next destination, Kasamatsu Park.  
You have to take a cable car to Kasamatsu Park, and the round trip cost was about 800 yen.  
I was getting hungry on the way, so I stopped at a place called Hashigoya that was serving oden.  
The interior looked fresh and stylish, as though the shop had opened recently. It had no Google reviews yet.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105041501191.webp" alt="image-20250105041501191" />  
  
They served craft beer and local sake.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105041622938.webp" alt="image-20250105041622938" />  
  
They even had flavor charts for their local sake and craft beer, which seemed helpful when choosing what to order.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105041630380.webp" alt="image-20250105041630380" />  
  
I ordered a six-piece oden set: radish, grilled tofu, kamaboko fish cake, grilled egg, konjac, and sausage. I also asked for a recommendation of hot local sake and ordered one go in a tokkuri flask. The oden was 1,500 yen and the sake 1,000 yen.  
The sake was Ine Mankai from Mukai Brewery, and it had a very distinctive flavor. Its color suggested rosé wine, and its acidity stood out too. I drank it hot, and it was excellent.  
The oden was quite tasty. On a chilly day, oden and hot sake made a wonderful combination.  
I searched again now and found 6 reviews. It seemed like a few other people had visited since I left the first one.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105042314354.webp" alt="image-20250105042314354" />  
  
I saw a strange sign near the cable car station and it said it was a power spot post...  
The detailed explanation is well written in Korean, so you can check it.  
(It was handwritten and translated by a person rather than a machine, which was rather touching.)  
I visited a nearby omiyage store and bought senbei and a postcard as souvenirs. I didn't write a letter and send it...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105042454022.webp" alt="image-20250105042454022" />

Panoramic view of Amanohashidate taken near the cable car platform at Kasamatsu Park.  
I feel a little disappointed that the curves of Amanohashidate are not clearly revealed, but I am a little moved by the thought that I came here to see this.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105042935184.webp" alt="image-20250105042935184" />

Looking closely, the advertisement characters were drawn by BUNBUN, the illustrator known for light novels such as Rose of Maria and Sword Art Online. Interesting to see. Is the brand pronounced Nariyoshi Sowaka...?  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105043253224.webp" alt="image-20250105043253224" />

<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105043457432.webp" alt="image-20250105043457432" />  
  
The view as evening approached.

There's a custom called matanozoki, looking at Amanohashidate upside down through your legs. I tried it too.  
Honestly... I think the normal view is better?  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105043724763.webp" alt="image-20250105043724763" />  
  
Strangely, there was a bus stop at the park on the mountain I climbed by cable car, so I took a picture.  
The bus seemed to go to the nearby Nariaiji Temple, but it was getting late, so I didn't visit.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105043803890.webp" alt="image-20250105043803890" />  
  
An advertisement at the cable car station mentioned a hotel offering day-use bathing. I thought I'd give it a try and headed over.  
It was Amanohashidate Hotel, on the other side of the sandbar from where I was.  
It was raining a bit heavily so I decided it would be too much to walk, so I took the bus.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105044008165.webp" alt="image-20250105044008165" />  
  
There is a free foot bath area inside the hotel, so I asked the staff if I could use it, but they said I couldn't use it because it was currently under repair. Too bad...  
The day-use bathing fee was 2,000 yen, as advertised, including a bath towel.  
The entrance to the main bath looks like this. The hotel overall has a clean and luxurious feel.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105044329947.webp" alt="image-20250105044329947" />  
  
Of course, photography was prohibited inside the bath, so I couldn’t take any pictures.  
One cold sauna and one hot sauna. It was a typical large bath attached to a hotel or ryokan, consisting of an internal hot spring, a cold bath, and an open-air hot spring.  
On the day I went, there were almost no customers, so I felt like I was renting it out.  
An outdoor hot spring bath in the rain has a special atmosphere I don't get to experience in Korea. Moments like this remind me why I travel to Japan. The spring water was clean and pleasant.  
There were plenty of cosmetics as advertised, though a few had run out, which was a little disappointing.  
At 2,000 yen, it wasn't great value, but I'd recommend it if you want a peaceful, quiet hot spring bath. I was satisfied.  
  
After taking a bath, I was a little hungry, so I looked for a store, but since it was a rural area, most of them were closed. Among the few stores that were open, I visited one that had a good rating on Google Maps.  
The store name is 310 amanohashidate . https://maps.app.goo.gl/BnG3wacbpySHd5V56  
First impression is that it is a high-end restaurant with a sophisticated interior.  
Other guests who came first were drinking wine and having a meal.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105045111783.webp" alt="image-20250105045111783" />  
  
I ordered a draft beer and looked through the menu. The otoshi appetizer was simmered radish with yuzu sauce.  
Radish with a bit of acidity thanks to the yuzu. It was delicious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105045200702.webp" alt="image-20250105045200702" />  
  
I ordered koppe-gani, female snow crab carrying roe, and gyokai paella-fu donabe gohan, seafood paella-style clay-pot rice.  
In addition, I ordered two types of Japanese sake recommended by the clerk.  
  
Koppe-gani is an Amanohashidate specialty, so I gave it a try. It was good.  
The amount looks a bit small, but it is full of crab roe, so I am quite satisfied.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105045409491.webp" alt="image-20250105045409491" />  
  
If you remove some of it, it feels like this. The combination of snow crab meat, roe, wasabi, and soy sauce was great.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105045836594.webp" alt="image-20250105045836594" />  
  
The pairing was Tamagawa Junmai Muroka Nama Genshu Yamahai, the 3u version.  
Although it wasn't a ginjo-grade sake, it tasted clean and sweet. The unfiltered, unpasteurized sake and yamahai brewing added a pleasant acidity that paired well with the cold crab.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105045646995.webp" alt="image-20250105045646995" />  
  
The pot rice came out 30 minutes after I ordered it (they warned me in advance that it would take a long time).  
Pot rice covered in seafood and vegetables. The seasoning was appropriate and delicious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105050003306.webp" alt="image-20250105050003306" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105050042458.webp" alt="image-20250105050042458" />  
  
With the rice, I had Yuho Junmai served warm.  
It was a dry sake with a sake meter value of about 5.3, and it worked well warmed up.  
Dry sake really does seem to pair better with rice.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250105050152739.webp" alt="image-20250105050152739" />  
  
The price came out to around the mid-7,000 yen, and it was a very satisfying dinner.  
The staff’s Japanese sake pairing sense was also good.  
Tamagawa is really delicious, so I highly recommend it.  
  
I decided to walk back across Amanohashidate to my accommodation and work off dinner along the way.  
It took approximately 50 minutes.  
Because it was night, the pictures came out a bit strange so I didn't take any, but it was like taking a walk at night.
