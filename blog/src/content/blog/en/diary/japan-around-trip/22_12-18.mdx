## Travel Diary  
  
### Morning hot spring bath  
  
#### Outdoor hot springs on the beach  
  
In winter, the open-air hot springs on the beach were open from 6:50 a.m. to 4:30 a.m. at sunset.  
I wanted to see the sunrise, so I woke up earlier than usual and went to the open-air hot springs around 7 a.m.  
  
On the way, there were quite a few signs posted prohibiting photography and video taking, as overseas tourists were causing quite a bit of trouble by taking pictures.  
I had to walk about 50 meters from the indoor to the open-air hot spring, but the wind was quite strong and the path was very cold.  
(I was only wearing a yukata, without any thermal layers...)  
  
There were two baths, one for mixed bathing and one for women only, and the temperatures were around 40 degrees and 37 degrees respectively.  
I quickly went to the hot springs to thaw my frozen body.  
I wanted to see the sunrise, but there were a lot of clouds and the weather was not so good, so it was not easy to see the sun.  
  
I wanted to relax and enjoy the warm hot springs while looking at the beach, but the snow and wind were so strong that it was difficult to stay there for long.  
According to Apple weather standards, it felt like -13 degrees Celsius, so I felt like my ears and face would freeze if I stayed any longer, so I escaped after about 20 to 30 minutes.  
  
Still, the experience of being so close to the beach and taking a hot spring bath while watching the waves was a completely new experience, so although I was disappointed that the weather was not good, I was satisfied.  
Since taking pictures was prohibited, I took pictures in the inner hallway on the way instead.  
It is attractive that you can see the rough waves up close, as shown in the photo, and even while taking a hot spring bath.  
(For reference, you can enjoy the Gukan hot spring and the open-air hot spring on the beach as a day bath.)

#### On-site hot springs  
  
There was nowhere to wash at the seaside open-air bath, so I went to the new building's large bath—the one I had used the previous evening—to wash up.  
The men's and women's baths were switched, so today I entered the place that was yesterday's women's bath.  
Perhaps because it was morning, the temperature of the bath was relatively high, and I was satisfied because I could see a pretty good view of the open-air hot spring.  
  
### breakfast and walk  
  
After washing up, I went to the restaurant to have breakfast.  
It was a typical 3-4 star hotel buffet breakfast, and I picked up some things I liked.  
What's special is that sashimi is provided.  
The overall taste was good, especially the sausage at 6 o'clock.

### Open-air hot springs on the beach before sunset  
  
I also wanted to see the sunset from the open-air hot springs on the beach, so I went back to the open-air hot springs on the beach around 4 p.m.  
Looking at the position of the sun, it seemed like it would be difficult to see the sunset until the open-air hot spring closed.  
And there were still a lot of clouds so it was hard to see the sun, but the view of the sun shining through the clouds was quite nice.  
It was still less cold than in the morning, so I enjoyed the hot spring bath for about 40 minutes before coming out.  
  
### Evening, tuna-cutting demonstration  
  
Since the tuna-cutting demonstration starts at 6 o'clock, I set dinner time at 5:30 and went to the restaurant at that time.  
The buffet menu was no different from yesterday.  
The tuna of the day was introduced in the kitchen, and I was able to witness the magnificence of the tuna.  
There is an introduction to tuna on the sign next to it, and it appears to be tuna caught in the Fukuura area.

There was still time until the disbanding show, so I had dinner first.  
Seafood nabe, negitoro flying fish roe rice bowl, other sashimi, etc.  
Additionally, I tried Tazawako beer, and it tasted more bitter and dry than typical Japanese beers such as Asahi and Sapporo.  
It wasn't to my taste, so I stopped after one bottle and didn't order another.

The tuna-cutting demonstration started at 6 o'clock.  
It was amazing because it was my first time seeing a tuna being dismantled in front of my eyes, and the skills seemed impressive, so it was quite enjoyable to watch.

Tuna disassembled and sliced ​​into sashimi by craftsmen.  
You can freely take Akami, Medium Belly, Large Belly, Negitoro, etc.  
They later served grilled meat from the tuna's head, but I was full and didn't take any.

I'm a bit full from what I ate earlier, so I'm content with just taking this much.

Akami was also good as there was no fishy taste at all.
