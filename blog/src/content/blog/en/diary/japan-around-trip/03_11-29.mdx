## Journal  
  
### Okayama  
  
I woke up around 7 a.m., got ready, and checked out around 8 a.m.  
I must have eaten quite a lot yesterday, because I still wasn't hungry. I kept breakfast simple with an onigiri and chicken from Lawson. Has Lawson's chicken become a little less tasty than it used to be?  
  
I planned to visit Korakuen Garden and Okayama Castle this morning, so I first went to Okayama Station, left my luggage, and took a tram to Shiroshita Station.  
The tram station is located about 200 m away from Okayama Station, and the panoramic view is as shown in the photo below.  
It is not common to see streetcars running around, so if you see one, I recommend riding it at least once.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104033957627.webp" alt="image-20250104033957627" />  
  
When you arrive at Shiroshita Station, you can go towards Korakuen & Okayama Castle through an underground passage. It was interesting that the underground passage was decorated like a plaza, so I took a picture.  
(Generally, there is a place like this in the center of the underground shopping mall, but it was a bit surprising that it was placed in a simple passageway.)  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034006594.webp" alt="image-20250104034006594" />  
  
I spotted Okayama Shrine on the way and only took a photo. I should have stopped in and drawn an omikuji...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034014415.webp" alt="image-20250104034014415" />  
  
It took about 15 minutes to walk from Shiroshita Station to the entrance of Korakuen near Tsurumi Bridge.  
Okayama Castle & Korakuen set ticket was on sale for 720 yen, so I bought it and entered Korakuen.  
There was also a pamphlet in Korean, so I grabbed it and walked around looking at the explanation in the pamphlet.  
There were free guided tours too, but unfortunately the timing didn't work out.  
  
My first impression of Korakuen was its expanse of lawn rather than elaborate planting. It felt more understated than some other Japanese gardens.  
I thought it was a well-constructed garden as it is one of the three major gardens in Japan.  
Inside were ponds, tea fields, Chinese-style fields, and an artificial hill overlooking the garden. Plenty of variety to enjoy.  
The weather was a little cloudy, but it was clear and it was fun to see the scenery.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034019857.webp" alt="image-20250104034019857" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034025537.webp" alt="image-20250104034025537" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034032818.webp" alt="image-20250104034032818" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034041119.webp" alt="image-20250104034041119" />  
  
After walking around Korakuen for about an hour and seeing everything, I headed south to Okayama Castle via Tsukimi Bridge.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034046965.webp" alt="image-20250104034046965" />  
  
Unlike many Japanese castles, Okayama Castle has a black keep. Apparently, its lord was related to Toyotomi Hideyoshi, so Hideyoshi's tastes influenced its appearance... or something like that.  
That's why the castle's nickname is Crow Castle.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034100288.webp" alt="image-20250104034100288" />  
  
A group of children, perhaps from a nursery, were on an outing in the castle grounds. It was a lovely scene, so I took a photo from a distance...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034106222.webp" alt="image-20250104034106222" />  
  
The inside of the castle tower consisted of a museum containing materials, artifacts, and exhibits related to Okayama Castle.  
I entered with a set ticket, but there was a Korean pamphlet so I grabbed one.  
The pamphlet material was a bit lacking, but instead, there was a virtual tour site that took pictures of the entire castle interior, so I walked around the inside of the castle tower while referring to that site.  
It was a little disappointing that only parts were translated into Korean. Still, I'd never seen somewhere offer this much, so I was rather impressed.  
https://okayama-castle.jp/virtualtour/ko/floor_5/tour.html  
The site is pretty good, so be sure to use it if you visit Okayama Castle.  
There were also several audio-visual materials inside, including a documentary about three people involved in Okayama Castle during the Battle of Sekigahara, a video in which a scholar explains Okayama Castle in detail, etc. I recommend it as it is quite fun to watch.  
  
As shown below, there was an activity where you could hold a gun and a Japanese sword, so I gave it a try.  
The weight wasn't that heavy.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034115083.webp" alt="image-20250104034115083" />  
  
After finishing my visit to Okayama Castle, I returned to Okayama Station to head to Kurashiki.  
  
### Kurashiki  
  
It was about 12:40 when I arrived at Kurashiki Station.  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104033843862.webp" alt="image-20250104033843862" />  
  
I was hungry and was wondering what to eat for lunch, but I found a good-looking ramen restaurant on Google Maps and headed there.  
Kurashiki Ramen Masuya. https://maps.app.goo.gl/XDmbtJm8HLt3Ptuv8  
It seems to be a restaurant that mainly sells niboshi ramen.  
I ordered Kurashiki niboshi ramen with spicy miso, spring onion, and chashu toppings. I added a half portion of fried rice as a lunch set and a local Kurashiki sake called Mannensetsu. The total was 2,000 yen.  
Niboshi ramen tasted like rich anchovy broth and was delicious. The aji tamago was properly soft-boiled and delicious, and the fried rice was also reasonably delicious.  
Mannensetsu tasted somewhere between futsushu and honjozo: an ordinary Japanese sake. As expected, sake doesn't go particularly well with ramen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104033857628.webp" alt="image-20250104033857628" />  
  
After eating, I walk along the shopping street, and the buildings in the shopping street are pretty, so it's fun to look around.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104033919525.webp" alt="image-20250104033919525" />  
  
Near the shopping street, there is a shrine called Achi Shrine, located at the top of Mt. Tsurugata, so I decided to go for a walk.  
There were quite a few stairs, so it was a good way to work off lunch.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104033909223.webp" alt="image-20250104033909223" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104033936272.webp" alt="image-20250104033936272" />  
  
Achi Shrine is a fairly old shrine that was founded about 1,700 years ago and is said to enshrine the Munakata Three Goddesses, who govern the safety of navigation, as its main deity....  
It seems like a quiet shrine without many worshipers...  
  
A visit to a shrine means it's time to draw an omikuji fortune.  
I drew a tonbodama glass-bead fortune (medium luck) and a dove fortune (small luck).  
Each shrine has different omikuji. They're fun both to draw and to look at, so I always end up trying them.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104033941576.webp" alt="image-20250104033941576" />  
  
In addition to this shrine, there is also a shrine dedicated to Bodhidharma, so I took a picture...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104033947609.webp" alt="image-20250104033947609" />  
  
I came down from Achi Shrine and continued walking along the shopping street. The sky was clear and the buildings were pretty, so it felt good to walk while appreciating the scenery.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034132913.webp" alt="image-20250104034132913" />  
  
While exploring the shopping street, I spotted Morita Sake Brewery and went straight in.  
Unfortunately, I wasn't able to taste it.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104044948047.webp" alt="image-20250104044948047" />  
  
After looking through the products, I bought an aged sake called Haten.  
It was Mannensetsu long-aged ginjo sake, matured for five years from 2019.  
I tried it two days later. The aging gave it a deep, clean flavor.  
I think it’s recommended if you like aged Japanese sake.  The price is 2008 yen (tax included)  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104045003733.webp" alt="image-20250104045003733" />  
  
The place to pay was the store next door, and the liqueur bottle was pretty, so I bought one. The price is a bit expensive...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104045017149.webp" alt="image-20250104045017149" />  
  
Leaving the shopping street and walking toward the outskirts, I found a restaurant doing a Demon Slayer collaboration.  
Demon Slayer really does so many collaborations... Well, it is popular.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034144668.webp" alt="image-20250104034144668" />  
  
While walking, I found an interesting building called Piggy Bank Museum with a dog on display on the roof, so I took a picture.  
It was a bit suspicious so I didn’t go in.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034152132.webp" alt="image-20250104034152132" />  
  
After walking for a while, I was thirsty and started looking for a cafe. A place called Amazake Yogurt Cafe caught my attention, so I went inside.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034158195.webp" alt="image-20250104034158195" />  
  
Strangely enough, there was a tasting machine inside where you could taste Jizake.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034211883.webp" alt="image-20250104034211883" />  
  
I ordered a sake tasting set (four glasses for 1,200 yen) and a Berry Berry Amazake with amazake whipped cream.  
For the tasting, I chose numbers 1, 2, 5, and 6 from the left in the photo. Number 2 was quite good, a little like sparkling white wine; the others were just so-so.  
The Berry Berry Amazake was a strawberry yogurt smoothie with a fairly strong bitter taste.  
It felt like an unlucky version of a strawberry yogurt smoothie, so I wouldn't recommend it...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034204015.webp" alt="image-20250104034204015" />  
  
After leaving the cafe, I visited the main street of Kurashiki Bikan District and immediately saw the scenery that appeared in the photo.  
Except for the buildings, the exotic scenery that didn't look like Japan was impressive.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034222238.webp" alt="image-20250104034222238" />  
  
I spotted a pretty building called the Kibibi Museum and took a photo. I didn't go in because admission was 700 yen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034303293.webp" alt="image-20250104034303293" />  
  
The cat in a fur coat in front of the store was cute.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034401497.webp" alt="image-20250104034401497" />  
  
A building inside Kurashiki Ivy Square that was apparently used as a G7 meeting venue...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034447089.webp" alt="image-20250104034447089" />  
  
If you walk around the Kurashiki Bikan District, you will see a lot of denim-related products on display. This is because Kurashiki City has had an active textile industry for a long time and is the birthplace of Japan's domestic jeans industry.  
I didn't buy anything because I thought it would be useless, but I was surprised to see wallets and dolls made of denim.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034518929.webp" alt="image-20250104034518929" />  
  
While walking around the Bikan District, I found a place where Japanese houses had been opened as an exhibition hall and decided to take a tour.  
It was the Inoue Residence. I was the only visitor when I arrived, so the 16th head of the family personally showed me around every corner.  
He explained the tea room, guest room, family rooms, kitchen, toilet, bath, and more. The windows, doors, and storage spaces particularly impressed me.  
It was interesting how the windows and doors were separated into 2 or 3 layers or combined. It's a shame I didn't take a video...  
There was a lot of storage space inside, and it was surprising that the structure of the storage space in the old mansion was not much different from the modern structure.  
Finally, he used a model to explain how the timber joints of the Inoue Residence fit together. I even got to take it apart and reassemble it myself. Really good. If you speak Japanese or English, I highly recommend it. Admission was 500 yen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034534373.webp" alt="image-20250104034534373" />  
  
Next, I visited the Ohara Residence. It was a little disappointing that there wasn't someone to guide me as closely as at the Inoue Residence.  
Still, it was pretty because there was a space at the entrance where the words left by the old heads of the family were displayed in a sophisticated manner.  
A staff member also took a photo. (I omitted the photo because it was a bit embarrassing)  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034549163.webp" alt="image-20250104034549163" />  
  
I also wanted to see the Bikan Historical Quarter at sunset. Since there was still time before the sun went down, I passed some time at the tourist information center, then enjoyed the evening scenery.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104035005671.webp" alt="image-20250104035005671" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104034823019.webp" alt="image-20250104034823019" />  
  
On the way, there was a space where Japanese umbrellas were displayed, as shown in the photo.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104035126599.webp" alt="image-20250104035126599" />

From Okayama Station, take the Shinkansen to Shin-Osaka Station.  
  
### Osaka  
  
My accommodation was near Namba, so I bought a one-day subway pass, rode over, and checked in.  
Staying in Osaka made sense for traveling toward Amanohashidate the next day, but the bigger reason was that I wanted to visit some izakayas I'd seen on YouTube.  
I had two places in mind. The first specialized in warayaki, straw-fire grilling.  
The rice straw grill looked so delicious that I went from Namba Station to Minamimorimachi Station and walked there.  
The name was Warayakibu Minamimorimachi. https://maps.app.goo.gl/9USFjREHcybsk5FG9  
Here's the related YouTube video. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eklOGfr9YEQ&t=881s  
  
It was packed with people, so after waiting for about 5 to 10 minutes, fortunately, I was able to get a seat.  
I was the only person dining alone, which felt a little awkward...  
I wouldn't particularly recommend visiting alone. Everyone else was in a group, and there were no seats for solo diners, so I felt a bit self-conscious.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250104033814734.webp" alt="image-20250104033814734" />  
  
First things first at an izakaya: a draft beer.  
I ordered chicken skin skewers, straw-grilled bonito, potato salad, straw-grilled saury, and straw-grilled pork jowl.  
I left out the pork jowl photo because it wasn't very presentable...  
The chicken skin was a bit disappointing, but the rest of the menu was all delicious.  
Warayaki has a straw scent, so it feels like it amplifies the taste a bit more?  
After beer, I ordered hot sake and chilled sake. Both were ordinary, so I don't remember their names. (I didn't make a note of them either.)  
Total amount about 5,800 yen? Passed here!

Although I was somewhat full at the Warayaki restaurant, I wanted to go to the next izakaya, so I continued my visit.  
The next place was Fukuriki Kitahama Sakaba. https://maps.app.goo.gl/x2oe6rgPduLBnsKp7  
Here's the related YouTube video. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DfwGnXlise4&t=1s  
  
I started with a draft beer and ordered the pork hotpot from the video, along with fried daikon and yakisoba.  
After sitting down and eating some yakisoba, I felt quite full.  
I didn't want to waste what I'd ordered, so I forced myself to keep eating...  
The butatetsu pork dish I'd most looked forward to was rather salty.  
Was the butatetsu only 330 yen? The portion was enormous for the price, so the value for money really was excellent.  
  
The total amount paid was 2,300 yen.  
The service was disappointing because the staff were busy, and the snacks in general didn't suit my taste, so I can't recommend it.  
The evaluation is okay...  
  
This was another izakaya that felt a little awkward to visit alone.  
Everyone else was in a group; I was the only solo diner.
