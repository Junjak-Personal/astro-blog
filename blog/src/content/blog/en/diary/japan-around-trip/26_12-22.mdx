### From Ueno Station to Sendai  
  
I had a tasting reservation at Nikka Miyagikyo Distillery in Sendai for 13:30, so I needed to leave early.  
I decided to have an ekiben for breakfast.  
I wanted to try it at least once because it was often portrayed as a romantic part of train travel in animations, dramas, and YouTube.  
After thinking about what to eat, I decided on an ekiben with tempura and grilled salmon.  
The taste was a bit odd because it was cold... It was 1164 yen, but it felt disappointing considering the price.  
  
Departed at 8:43 and arrived at 10:15.  
After all, Shinkansen... It's fast, but it's too expensive.  
  
When I arrived at Sendai Station, it was snowing, and unlike Tokyo, people were dressed warmly, so I thought I had come to the north.  
If you go to the main station in the area, first go to the tourist information center.  
I was curious to see an unknown dinosaur dressed as Santa in front of the information center, so I took a picture of it.  
There was still time to get to the distillery, so I looked around the station, stopped by Yodobashi Camera, took a look, and stopped at the accommodation to store my luggage.  
  
Miyagikyo Distillery can be reached by shuttle bus from nearby JR Sakunami Station, so I went to Sakunami Station first.  
There was so much snow outside the car window...  
  
Finally arrived at Sakunami Station.  
The snowfall was no joke.

### Tasting course  
  
Number ticket received for paid tasting.  
This is a great course that allows you to taste 5 different types of whiskey for 1,500 yen, so if you come to Miyagikyo Distillery, be sure to make a reservation and experience it.  
  
Below are 5 whiskeys to taste.  
First, the course includes an explanation of Nikka Whiskey, followed by a tour of the distillery and a tasting at the end.  
I'm looking forward to tasting it.  
  
Since the course itself was a Japanese language course, all materials and explanations were in Japanese.  
Since the content is not very difficult, I think I can easily understand it at N2 level.  
  
Nikka has two distilleries, and has opened a new Miyagikyo distillery in Sendai to create blended whiskey by mixing whiskeys with different tastes.  
Although it was built in 1969, it was well maintained and repaired, so there was no impression that it was old at all.

### Distillery tour  
  
After listening to the explanation about Nikka, I took a full-scale tour of the distillery.  
Although it was no longer snowing outside, it snowed a lot and I was able to see the landscape of the distillery covered in snow.  
All the clouds had disappeared and the clear sky and snow-covered scenery of the distillery gave an exotic feeling, as if I was at a Scottish distillery.  
  
During the tour, I had the opportunity to actually see and touch Pete.  
Peat is known for its distinctive aroma, but the peat itself had almost no smell.  
Is it because all the scent has been released?  
  
You could actually see these fermentation tanks, a huge copper still, and even a whiskey cellar.  
  
There was also a description of the types of wooden barrels used to store whiskey.

### Tasting  
  
After touring the distillery, I headed to the information center for a tasting.  
  
Finally, the tasting started.  
After receiving guidance from the guide, I begin tasting according to the tasting method and tasting order.  
  
Below are my thoughts on the whiskey I tasted.  
  
- Miyagikyo: This was the first one I tried, and the initial flavor was sweet. Its brandy-like sweetness was memorable.  
- Yoichi: Compared to Miyagi Kyo, it was a whiskey with a rougher, more peat flavor.  
- Taketsuru: It was a blend of Miyagikyo and Yoichi whiskey, and it was a taste that allowed you to feel the essence of Nikka, bringing out the best of both worlds. The most delicious whiskey  
- Coffey Grain: It is distilled in a Coffey still, but it did not leave much of an impression on me.  
- Frontier: A blended whiskey with a slightly soft feel, but the aftertaste was short.  
  
That's how I ended the tasting, and this year is Nikka's 90th anniversary.  
I felt good about coming on such a meaningful day.

### Sushi Yoshikane — Sakizuke to Suimono  
  
When it was time, I went to Sushi Yoshikane for dinner.  
I made a reservation two months ago. The price is 13,200 yen, which is the most expensive of the omakase menus, but it also has a variety of menus other than omakase, so it was a place where you could eat comfortably.  
  
When I sat down, I was handed a piece of paper with today's course written on it.  
When eating omakase or course meals in Japan, you usually hear the explanation verbally, but it is often difficult to hear because it is drowned out by surrounding sounds and the names of the ingredients are difficult to understand.  
However, I was really impressed that the course structure was written in text like this.  
Additionally, they had the sense to write the name of the person making the reservation at the top. It feels like you are being treated well.  
(My friend is also Mr. Yoon, so by coincidence the course description felt appropriate.)  
  
The interior of the store uses a lot of gold, creating a luxurious and sophisticated atmosphere.  
It was cool to see the chef working hard on preparing the food over the counter.  
  
The alcohol menu also had a variety of local sake from Miyagi Prefecture.  
  
Perhaps because I arrived on time, it took about 10 minutes for the first menu to appear.  
Meanwhile, the master asked if I would like a drink of sake as a welcome drink, so I got one.  
Houyou usunigori maiodoru junmai namagenshu (鳳陽 うすにごり 舞踊る 純米 生原酒).  
It was a Japanese liquor with a sweet spec of -8, and because it was namagenshu + nigori, it had a slight makgeolli feel.  
  
The first dish, sakizuke, was served with cabbage and mushroom ohitashi.  
It had just the right amount of acidity, making it the perfect starter to whet your appetite.  
I'd already finished the welcome drink, so I ordered one go of hot sake (atsukan, 熱燗) on their recommendation, hoping to warm up after the cold outside.  
It was Houyou Junmaishu. With a sake meter value of +3 and a rice-polishing ratio of 65%, it had a clean flavor that worked well served hot.  
  
Next, suimono (吸物), Hamaguri (clam) clear soup.  
It contained nama-fu (生麩, wheat-gluten cake) and vegetables. The flavor was a little mild, but it wasn't bad alongside the hot sake.

### Sushi Yoshikane — Sashimi to Yakimono  
  
Three types of authentic sashimi — medium toro, matsukawa karei (high-quality flounder), and tsubu clam.  
Everything was fresh and delicious. In particular, Matsukawa Garei has an impressive chewy texture.  
  
Next, grilled yellowtail Sakekasu with shinogi, pickled shad gizzard gizzard, and pickled kelp wrapped in kelp were served.  
Among these, the most shocking one was **smoked yellowtail (ブリ) sushi**.  
It was my first time seeing it smoked and served like this, and the combination of the smoky flavor and the soft taste and texture of yellowtail was truly amazing.  
The most memorable point of today's entire course.  
  
Yakimono (焼物) is kamasu (カマス, skewered meat) grilled with salt.  
It was a simple grilled fish with only salt, and it was a dish that proved that if the ingredients are good, a simple recipe is the answer.  
  
The next pairing is Hidakami junmai Shu from Ishinomaki City, Miyagi Prefecture.  
It seemed to go well with seafood with just the right amount of karaguchi.

### Sushi Yoshikane — Mushimono to the finish  
  
Mushimono is chawanmushi topped with salmon roe.  
Chawanmushi is common in Korean Japanese restaurants these days, so is it a common taste without much differentiation?  
It's delicious.  
  
Makimono (巻物) is Tekkamaki (鉄火巻き), a mixture of various colors.  
The same sushi rice as sushi shari was used, so you could feel the texture of the rice, and the fresh tuna was also delicious.  
  
Agemono (揚物) include anago (穴子, conger eel) and eggplant.  
Anago, properly cooked in a thin batter, was an excellent Japanese-style tempura that brought out both texture and taste.  
This fried food is also one of the highlights of the day.  
  
Sunomono (酢物) with shirako (white fish) and cod liver seasoned with ponzu.  
It's a combination that often appears in omakase, but I'm not sure if it's delicious yet...  
It seems like internal organs are still difficult..?  
  
Next came clam sushi,  
This is also high-quality shellfish, and although it is delicious, I still prefer fish sushi.  
  
Finally, a salmon roe and uni rice bowl—though 'bowl' is a stretch, as it was only slightly larger than a piece of sushi...  
Since you can't go wrong with seafood combined with sushi rice, it feels like an appropriate ending, right?  
  
I was able to taste a variety of local sake from Miyagi Prefecture, and I could feel the sincerity in each dish that was served, so I thought I would definitely visit again the next time I come to Sendai.  
The master and staff were very kind, and I was really grateful that they asked me questions first and treated me kindly since I was a foreigner from Korea.  
  
After a delicious dinner, return to the accommodation and take a hot spring bath.
