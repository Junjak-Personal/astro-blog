## Journal  
  
Today I decided to go from Kanazawa to Nagoya Station to meet friends I was planning to travel with.  
After having breakfast at the hotel I stayed at, I checked out and took the bus from the hotel to Kanazawa Station.  
I boarded the Shinkansen bound for Tsuruga at 10:04 and arrived at Nagoya Station via Tsuruga -> Maibara at 12:25.  
  
We decided to meet at around 12:30 in front of the Gold Clock, a famous meeting place at Nagoya Station, and have lunch first.  
We went to Nagoya Station's Ramen Umaimondori and picked a place that looked suitable.  
  
The selected restaurant is Fukukita Ramen Kuramachi.  
https://maps.app.goo.gl/bPxR6jzQTJpiG1Tr6  
It specialized in shoyu ramen. I ordered the special shoyu ramen and stir-fried chicken skin, for a total of 1,450 yen.  
The shoyu ramen wasn't especially tasty. My friends didn't seem particularly satisfied either.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111024023185.webp" alt="image-20250111024023185" />  
  
After lunch, we went to the subway at Nagoya Station, bought 760-yen 24-hour passes, and headed to our accommodation to check in.  
We were staying at Cafe & Guesthouse Nagonoya in the Endoji shopping street.  
I didn't take photos, but the guesthouse was in Nagonoya's annex, down a side alley.  
There was a lounge and the interior facilities were good, but the problem was that the room was too narrow.  
There were three people sleeping, but there was almost no space to put luggage except for bedding.  
  
First, I roughly packed my bags and walked to my next destination, Nagoya Castle.  
I visited Nagoya Castle quite a few times as I studied abroad at Nagoya University in the past, and there was nothing new there, so I didn't take many pictures.  
  
I went back inside the restored Honmaru Palace for the first time in a while.  
Rather than the famous castle tower, I looked around Nagoya Castle, paying attention to the newly installed exhibits, which were different from my previous visits.  
In the past, I wasn't very interested in Japanese history, so I just saw it as a famous tourist area, but after learning a little about the history of the Warring States Period, there were some parts that I saw in a new way.  
Nagoya Castle was built by Tokugawa Ieyasu to pressure Toyotomi Hideyori and to serve as a strategic point for Tokaido, and that the Owari Tokugawa family ruled the castle as the lords of the castle for 260 years.

After looking around Nagoya Castle, the group said they wanted to stop by Uniqlo and GU .  
I took the subway and went to Nagoya Station JR Tower.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111030000441.webp" alt="image-20250111030000441" />  
  
After stopping at Starbucks at the tower and quenching my thirst, I went to GU and Uniqlo.  
The group bought some clothes, and I bought some underwear.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111030034488.webp" alt="image-20250111030034488" />  
  
After brief shopping at GU , I moved to Bic Camera on the lower floor.  
The group was also interested in alcohol, so we first went to the alcohol corner of Bic Camera.  
  
At Bic Camera, I found Kleiner, which a radio show had described as a drink for party people, and bought one.  
It tasted perhaps slightly better than flavored soju like Soonhari. In other words, I wasn't impressed.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111030238477.webp" alt="image-20250111030238477" />  
  
Next, they were selling cocktails, so I took a picture because I was curious.  
It was my first time seeing the cocktail itself in a market rather than a bar, and the packaging was also unique.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111030446636.webp" alt="image-20250111030446636" />  
  
Afterwards, I looked around the home appliances, audio equipment, gaming corner, etc.  
We moved to Sakae Station to go to the next destination, Oasis 21.|  
Oasis 21 is often used as an event venue, but this time, the ice rink was used as the main venue.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111030818324.webp" alt="image-20250111030818324" />  
  
First, when we entered our destination, Nigirino Tokube Conveyor Belt Sushi, there was a bit of a wait.  
 https://maps.app.goo.gl/VpkXK8gqgX7svkba8  
After waiting for about 20 minutes, I was shown to a counter seat, and surprisingly, the waiter spoke Korean quite well.  
Thanks to this, my companions were able to receive guidance without difficulty.  
  
I felt this way when I was in Nagoya before, but strangely enough, this is a store that many Koreans visit.  
This time too, there were 3 to 4 Korean teams.  
I ordered a trio of yellowtail sushi, conger eel, red sea bream, and other pieces, eating about 2,900 yen's worth.  
It was a fairly ordinary but tasty conveyor-belt sushi place. The conger eel's sauce was good enough to recommend.  
Considering I was going to an izakaya, I ate a little less, but I was still somewhat full.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111031024391.webp" alt="image-20250111031024391" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111031249936.webp" alt="image-20250111031249936" />  
  
After eating, I went outside and saw a local idol performing on the stage in front of the ice rink.  
Of course, I was able to see it from off stage, but  
Chances to see a show up close like this aren't common, so after discussing it with my friends, we paid 800 yen and entered the performance area.  
  
A sign prohibited photography, video, and audio recording. But everyone filmed the performers greeting the audience after the show, and the staff didn't seem to stop them, so perhaps that part was allowed.  
My impression of the performance was, "They're working hard..." The audience's lukewarm response was a little disappointing.

<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111031858518.webp" alt="image-20250111031858518" />  
  
After dinner, my original destination was to go to a place called Tebasaki Mutsumi, a Tebasaki restaurant that was delicious the last time I went there, but I failed to enter because I didn't make a reservation...  
While we were looking around, a street promoter led us to a nearby izakaya.  
Actually, it was a store I visited last time, and it wasn't bad in my opinion, but my companions' reviews said it was the worst place.  
So I didn't take any pictures...  
After drinking a glass of draft beer and some snacks, I stood up. The total amount spent is 1,300 yen.  
  
Since it was quite a while, I returned to the accommodation.  
This is what Endoji shopping street looks like at night. Except for a few izakayas, all are closed.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111032352455.webp" alt="image-20250111032352455" />  
  
I don't know if it was a teahouse, but there was a performance on the way to my accommodation, so I enjoyed it a bit.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111032430774.webp" alt="image-20250111032430774" />  
  
One friend was tired and went inside. The other friend and I weren't ready to call it a night, so we looked for another izakaya,  
Go to “Endoji Yatai Delicious Oasis” near Endoji shopping street.  
https://maps.app.goo.gl/oz3iPs9ejqhvrJYD9  
  
It mainly catered to locals. When we arrived as Korean tourists, I got the impression that we weren't particularly welcome.  
The menu was also handwritten, so it was impossible to interpret it with a translator.  
First, I received a recommendation from the clerk and ordered the Aka version of braised pork cartilage, pollack roe omelet, and highball.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111032748376.webp" alt="image-20250111032748376" />  
  
Braised pork cartilage tastes like gamjatang, perfect for the taste of Koreans. The price is not expensive at 800 yen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111032913443.webp" alt="image-20250111032913443" />  
  
The pollack roe omelette was a bit salty, but it wasn't bad. 680 yen  
The tebasaki I added seemed more like chicken drumettes than whole wings. They tasted good. 550 yen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111033302878.webp" alt="image-20250111033302878" />  
  
Next was fried takoyaki. It felt like reheated frozen takoyaki. 380 yen.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111033431340.webp" alt="image-20250111033431340" />  
  
The last thing I ordered was stir-fried pork kimchi, a combination you can't go wrong with.  
It came properly topped with an egg yolk.  
Just watching the pork belly being cooked and stir-fried on an iron plate looked delicious, and it was actually delicious.  
I'd already thought so with the earlier dishes: this place really can cook.  
  
Unlike Korean kimchi, Japanese kimchi has a sweeter taste that stands out more.  
Maybe that's why this stir-fried pork kimchi also tastes quite sweet.  
However, the saltiness is added due to other seasonings, making it the ultimate sweet-salty combination.  
The pork belly is cooked just the way Koreans like it.  
  
Additionally, I ordered sour and Japanese sake. The price was 7,260 yen for two people.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250111033537510.webp" alt="image-20250111033537510" />  
  
At first, I got the feeling that the master was displeased with Koreans.  
After I ordered in Japanese and talked a bit with the other customers, that feeling immediately disappeared.  
I guess it's probably because it's difficult and annoying to deal with foreigners.  
  
This is a highly recommended restaurant if you can read and order the menu in Japanese.  
The atmosphere has a local feel and, above all, the food is delicious.
