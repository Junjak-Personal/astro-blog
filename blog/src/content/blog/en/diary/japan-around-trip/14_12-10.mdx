## Schedule  
  
Yesterday, I was unable to leisurely look around the Miyagawa morning market to catch the Shirakawa-go bus.  
I decided to finish exploring the parts I'd missed this morning.  
Yesterday I mainly looked around the street stalls outside, but today I mainly looked around the souvenir shop inside the store.  
They were selling a lot of special products unique to Takayama, such as unique dolls and Hobamiso.  
I lost my umbrella in Toyama, so I bought an umbrella with a Japanese sword-shaped handle.

The taste is ordinary Oyakodon and Shoyu base ramen.

The food was decent too, making it a good stop on a Your Name location tour.

The festival supplies warehouse, Furukawa Matsuri Hall, and Hida-Furukawa Station railway seen along the way.  
The sky was cloudy on the Lawson side, but as we moved to the other side, toward the shrine, the sky became clearer.

Even though it's not far from a private house and it's a park, they said to be careful about bears...

Ketawakamiya Shrine is famous as a shrine for Your Name, but it is also the shrine that was originally the center of the Furukawa Festival.  
It is considered an important shrine in the region.  
The main deity is Onamuchinokami (大己貴神), who is considered the god of medicine, agriculture, and commerce.  
  
Omikuji to commemorate coming to the shrine.  
There was only a regular omikuji, so I paid 100 yen and got one, but the result was good.  
Additionally, there are amulets related to the 12 zodiac signs, and as someone born in the year of the rat, I purchased a rat amulet. 500 yen.  
  
On the way down from the shrine, you could see a panoramic view of Hida-Furukawa.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115061305783.webp" alt="image-20250115061305783" />

The itinerary in Hida-Furukawa ended with taking pictures of the bus stop and Hida-Furukawa Station that appeared in “Your Name.”  
When I went back to Takayama, I took the limited express Hida.

I discovered it after completing my Takayama itinerary, so I was disappointed that I couldn't participate in the event.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115061744374.webp" alt="image-20250115061744374" />  
  
Putting that disappointment behind me, I caught a bus from Takayama Bus Terminal to my next destination, Hirayu in Okuhida Onsen-go.  
Arrived in about an hour.  
It is located in the Hida Mountains, called the Northern Alps of Japan, so the altitude was quite high at 1300 m .  
I saw many ski resorts on the way.  
  
My original main goal had been hiking in Kamikochi, but the usual access roads close in winter,  
buses don't run, and entry requires specialist equipment such as snowshoes.  
So I gave up on Kamikochi and changed the plan to sightseeing in Hida-Furukawa and relaxing in Hirayu's hot springs.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115062008768.webp" alt="image-20250115062008768" />  
  
When I went into the souvenir shop inside, I was able to see another character from Onsen Musume that I had seen in Takayama.  
Her name was Hirayu Mitsuba. I bought a pin badge as a souvenir.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115062331373.webp" alt="image-20250115062331373" />  
  
There was merchandise combining the Hida mascot with anime characters too. I bought a Demon Slayer Nezuko keychain.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115062428287.webp" alt="image-20250115062428287" />  
  
After a quick look around the souvenir shop by the bus stop, I went to check in.  
The accommodation is Oyado Eitaro, a hot spring inn that is a bit cheaper in Hirayu.  
Still, since it is an inn, it costs 15,000 yen per person per night...

Through the hallway

Enter the room. This is a typical Japanese ryokan room.  
Unfortunately, there is no shower facility inside the room.

By hanging a sign like the picture below, you can freely use the inside without worrying about others.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115063330027.webp" alt="image-20250115063330027" />

The explanation said one of the components was nine times that of Gero Onsen, but I can't remember which...  
I decided to take a hot spring bath at the ryokan in the evening after looking around Hirayu Street.

Hirayu is a town centered on hot springs, with few shops other than the surrounding hot spring inns.  
Perhaps due to the closure of Kamikochi during the winter, there were not many tourists.  
The inn site and road are impressive in that the snow melts with hot spring water.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115063846771.webp" alt="image-20250115063846771" />  
  
On the way, I saw Onsen Tamago on sale at a nearby store, so I bought one. The price is 100 yen  
The egg whites also had a nice undercooked feel.

The entrance torii covered in snow was impressive.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115064329018.webp" alt="image-20250115064329018" />

Perhaps it was because there wasn't a staffed shrine office. Quite unusual.

On Google Maps, it is called Hirayu Folk Museum Ashiyu.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115064453930.webp" alt="image-20250115064453930" />  
  
Old Toyosaka residence registered on Google Maps.  
The icicles on the eaves were impressive, but the interior was dark and full of miscellaneous things, as if it had not been maintained.  
Probably not somewhere you need to make a special trip to visit?

Next, Hirayu Folk Museum.  
There were still traces of people here, but there were no people and it was dark inside.  
A gassho-zukuri style building like Shirakawa-go.  
The photo was taken with a flash, and there was also something that looked like a bearskin mat. There were many props.  
I couldn't figure out how to turn on the lights, so I just looked around briefly and left. There was no heating, and it was cold too...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115064537947.webp" alt="image-20250115064537947" />

Next, I went to Hirayu no Yu.  
This is a place where you can take a hot spring bath for free.  
It looks like it's open for tourists? The manager was nowhere to be seen.  
There are separate baths for men and women, and towels are not provided, so you need to bring them separately.  
The inside is a fairly large open-air bath, so I didn't take any pictures because there were other users.  
I think it's worth a visit as it feels like an untouched, untouched hot spring. It's free too...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115064647735.webp" alt="image-20250115064647735" />

Next is Hirayu Onsen Ashiyu Park, where you can enjoy a foot bath.  
Unlike what we saw earlier, this is a place where traces of management are clearly visible.  
I also tried a foot bath here.

By this time, the sun had almost set, and the view of the cafe from outside had its own charm.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115064851755.webp" alt="image-20250115064851755" />

The owner seemed to like collecting Cola containers, so they were displayed separately.  
Among them, there is also Korean Coca-Cola, so it feels familiar...  
I ordered whipped cream cocoa on the menu, and it tasted like ordinary cocoa. It was enough to warm my body.

Hirayu Shrine at night also had charm.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115064927725.webp" alt="image-20250115064927725" />  
  
It wasn't until night that I found out that the Hida mascot, a ninja, was on display on the second floor of the Hirayu Folk Museum.  
Gassho-zukuri Hirayu Folk Museum under the moonlight. It was a nice scenery.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115064944575.webp" alt="image-20250115064944575" />  
  
Then I headed toward Hirayu no Mori.  
With Kamikochi off the cards, Hirayu no Mori had been my second main goal. Unfortunately, it was closed too.  
Oyado Eitaro's landlady said it often closes in early December for maintenance and renovation work.  
Still, I wanted to see it with my own eyes, but it was closed as expected. From December 2nd to December 12th.  
I wish I had come here a little later.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115065004717.webp" alt="image-20250115065004717" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115065013509.webp" alt="image-20250115065013509" />  
  
After returning to the accommodation and enjoying a hot spring bath, I went out to have dinner.  
In a village of ryokans, there weren't many places to have dinner. I went into Ramen Sakaba Yadorigi.  
It was one of the places the landlady had recommended, since I hadn't booked dinner at the ryokan.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115065027604.webp" alt="image-20250115065027604" />  
  
In addition to the menu items, there was also today's recommended menu on the blackboard.  
I didn't notice this and ordered the main menu of Gyusuji Ramen, which was on the menu, with chashu and egg added.  
If I had known, I would have ordered Taiwanese ramen...  
In addition to ramen, there was also a simple alcohol menu.  
Craft beer is the main thing, plus simple cocktails, sours, and about 4 types of whiskey?  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115065059630.webp" alt="image-20250115065059630" />  
  
There were many fish tanks inside the store, and the owner was also posting a fish tank diary on YouTube.  
The latest video was a few months old, but he seemed to upload fairly regularly.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115065038924.webp" alt="image-20250115065038924" />  
  
I ordered the beef tendon ramen I'd mentioned, a craft beer, a cheese-and-potato mochi croquette, and ika no kurozukuri.  
The ramen and beer were usually delicious, but Ikanokuro-zukuri was very salty, so I can't recommend it.  
It feels like raw squid mixed in squid ink, but eating salted squid is much better.  
I wanted to talk to the owner, but he didn't come out of the kitchen, so I just ate alone quietly and left the store.  
  
Total amount spent is 3935 yen.  
If it were anywhere else, it wouldn't be a store I would highly recommend, but since there are no options in Hirayu, it's a good place to grab a quick meal.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115065138190.webp" alt="image-20250115065138190" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115065146272.webp" alt="image-20250115065146272" />  
  
After dinner, I was full, so I went for a walk in the direction of Hirayunomori.  
As expected from a heavy snowfall area, there was quite a lot of snow, but it was a bit surprising as it was my first time seeing more than 1 m of snow.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115065201533.webp" alt="image-20250115065201533" />  
  
The combination of alpine area + countryside + lack of light allowed me to see quite a few stars in the sky.  
My hometown is pretty remote too, but I'd never seen more stars than back home until now.  
I ended the day enjoying the night scenery.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250115065215722.webp" alt="image-20250115065215722" />
