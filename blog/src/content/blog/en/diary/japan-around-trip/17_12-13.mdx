## Travel Diary  
  
### Snow Monkey Park  
  
Today is the day I go to the long-awaited Jigokudani Wild Monkey Park.  
For this day, I purchased a Snow Monkey pass in advance yesterday.  
You can get there by taking an express bus from the bus terminal near Nagano Station.  
It's about 1800 yen one way, but it's free if you have a Snow Monkey Pass.  
  
Because of the bus schedule, I woke up a little early and went out to have breakfast at the hotel.  
Since it was a typical hotel breakfast, I didn't take any pictures of the food.  
Unusually, they were providing iminashiki hiyaoroshi for free.  
I tried one glass of sake, but it didn't feel particularly special.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-25_01.13.04.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-25_01.13.04" />  
  
After breakfast, take the bus at around 8:30 for about an hour and a half. It took about a while to arrive at the Snow Monkey Park stop.  
The streets are a bit snowy and slippery and dangerous.  
It took about 45 minutes to walk from the stop to the park.  
  
I saw a hot spring ryokan at the beginning of the way, and it made me want to stay there at least once.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-24_22.45.06.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-24_22.45.06" />  
  
Next was the shrine-like site by the entrance: Kanbayashi Fudoson. It enshrines a Buddhist object of worship, yet looks like a Shinto shrine, which I found interesting.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-24_22.46.17.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-24_22.46.17" />  
  
Next was the cafe seen at the entrance. This is the last resting place you can stop by before going to the park.  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-24_22.51.05.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-24_22.51.05" />  
  
Now I am entering the trekking course to the park in earnest, and since the icy road is dangerous near the entrance, there is a place that rents various equipment for a fee.  
Real men don't do things like that. There were many people who didn't do it,  
It seemed to be used a lot by families traveling with children.  
  
The trekking course feels like this.  
The mystical feeling of walking among tall coniferous trees covered in snow. You have to walk this way for about 30 minutes.  
Looking at the reviews, there were quite a few people saying that it was difficult because of the distance, but I enjoyed the view because I liked the scenery, so the time passed quickly.  
There was also a warning sign about falling rocks, and it actually felt like there was going to be a small avalanche or falling rock, so it was a little scary.

Although the buildings looked a bit old, I thought it would be nice to stay in a place like this.

I finally arrived at the monkey park.  
Since I had a Snow Monkey Pass, I didn't pay any admission fee. The entrance fee is about 800 yen.  
Since re-entry was not possible, the pass was stamped to show entry.  
There weren't many people on the trekking course, but the park was somewhat crowded with tourists.

It was a little disappointing that I couldn't bathe with the monkeys as depicted in various media, but when else would I get to see a scene like this...

It was nice to see a variety of things along the way, including monkeys fighting each other and monkeys sleeping.  
According to park security personnel, the monkeys do not have a home to call home, but instead go to the mountains to look for food and then come down here to enjoy hot spring baths.  
They are intelligent, but apparently have no particular urge to make a home, and aren't fussy about where they go to the toilet.  
The reason is because there is no need for that.  
  
I think it is a tourist destination that makes traveling a long distance worthwhile.  
On my way out, I bought an iron badge as a souvenir, passed the trekking course again, and took the bus back to Nagaden Yudanaka Station.

The character below is Shinshu Nakano-chan, a Vtuber created in Nakano City. Do you feel like twins with the black-haired Yoru Nakano?

I searched Google to see if there was a sake brewery nearby, and found Igaya Kuramoto, who brews the Iwashimizu label.  
I visited because the Google reviews were pretty good and I could taste it.  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-24_23.34.29.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-24_23.34.29" />  
  
The staff member (I found out later that it was Toji's wife) said it was 500 yen for 4 cups, so I paid the price and started tasting.  
As written in the Google review, the four signature-level sakes below are all relatively expensive, priced around 5,000 yen for 720ml.  
I regretted forgetting to photograph all of them. The photo below shows Mars and Gowaringo.  
  
- Gowaringo  
  - sake with the pink label in the photo below  
  - Normal sake uses about 20% rice in kojimai, but this sake uses 50% rice.  
  - It feels like eating sake with apple flavor added, as the name suggests, rather than the regular sake flavor. A bit like whitefine?  
  - It is also called Gowaringo because it smells like 50% kojimai and apples.  
  - It has a moderate acidity and is very good for stimulating the appetite. I really wanted to buy it, but I gave up because I had too much luggage.  
  - Personal rating: 4.5 / 5  
- Origin  
  - It had a pleasant, moderate acidity and tasted good, but the flavor, acidity, and aroma all felt a little less satisfying than Gowaringo.  
  - Personal rating: 3.7 / 5  
- Mars  
  - As the name suggests, this is part of a planetary series that also includes Jupiter and Earth.  
  - Like Gowaringo, it uses 50% of the rice as koji rice.  
  - You can feel the apple scent and acidity like Gowaringo, but Gowaringo tastes better.  
  - Personal rating: 3.9 / 5  
- Eclipse  
  - Sake aged for two years in a dark room at -5 degrees.  
  - Aging in a dark room kept it clear, without browning.  
  - The body has increased as it matures.  
  - Personal rating: 4 / 5  
  
All the sake I tried was good, and four glasses of signature-level sake for 500 yen was excellent value, so...  
If you ever visit Nakano City, I highly recommend stopping by.

### Shibu Onsen & Yuda Onsen  
  
Nagano is also famous for its hot springs, so I wanted to visit a hot spring and stopped by Shibu Onsen.  
To Nagaden, I took the train from Shinshu Nakano Station and stopped at Yudanaka Station.  
The area near Yudanaka Station is the Yuda Onsen area, which is also a famous hot spring area.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.45.12.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.45.12" />  
  
There was a place that charged 500 yen for bathing in Higaeri.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.45.22.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.45.22" />  
  
After looking around for a bit, I took a bus to Shibu Onsen.  
Overall, it is an old-style hot spring inn, and the atmosphere of the street was friendly and nice.  
Most of the people were tourists staying here, and there were many people walking around in Yutaka clothes.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.46.00.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.46.00" />  
  
I stopped by the hot spring information center and asked if there were any hot springs here where I could bathe for a day. They informed me that only bath number 9 in Shibu Onsen Meguri was open for daily bathing.  
The entrance fee was 800 yen. From October 1, 24, the fee increased from 500 yen to 800 yen...  
Bath number 9, where you can bathe during the day, is usually locked, but if you ask a nearby store or inn, they will open it.  
Onsen Meguri has a total of baths numbered 1 to 9, and the qualification is to stay at a hot spring inn in Shibu Onsen.  
Since I was a one-day tourist, I was a little disappointed that I couldn't experience Onsen Meguri.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.54.41.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.54.41" />  
  
That’s how we entered bath number 9. There was no one inside so I took a picture.  
  
The changing area and bath were divided by wooden boards, but it felt almost like a single shared room.  
Please note that towels are not provided, there are no shower facilities, and of course, it is prohibited to touch the bathtub with your hair.  
I thought 800 yen was a bit of a waste of money for this configuration.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.49.51.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.49.51" />  
  
The temperature of the internal bath feels a bit hot, about 42 to 43 degrees.  
You can adjust the temperature by turning on cold water through the faucet, but I thought this temperature was just right, so I didn't adjust the temperature separately.  
The hot spring feels like just a hot spring...  
I like to bathe in hot springs, so I've been to many places here and there, but to be honest, I don't know much about the difference in the water other than whether it's slippery or something depending on its composition, and as someone who thinks atmosphere is important, I felt a little disappointed that this wasn't an open-air hot spring.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.50.16.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-25_00.50.16" />

### Nagano (dinner)  
  
After breakfast at the hotel in the morning, I only ate a few snacks and didn't eat anything, so I was quite hungry.  
While looking for a store, I found the tempura and teuchi soba restaurant Sawano An.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-25_01.05.05.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-25_01.05.05" />  
  
There were quite a few types of alcoholic beverages, including sake and shochu, but since I was only interested in sake, I looked at the sake menu and there was the famous Jikon.  
The price is also cheap at 1050 yen.  
I couldn't resist this and immediately inquired with the staff. They told me that in addition to Jikon Tokubetsu junmai, they also had nigori, so I ordered both.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-25_01.04.53.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-25_01.04.53" />  
  
That's how I received Jikon Tokubetsu Junmai Nama and Jikon Tokubetsu Junmai Nigori Nama.  
It was a truly delicious sake that did not tarnish Jikon's reputation.  
Especially Nigori. I was a little dissatisfied with Nigorazake, thinking it tasted like makgeolli minus the carbonation.  
Jikon’s Nigorizake was a little different.  
Since it is Nama, it has a carbonated feel so it really feels like you are eating makgeolli, but the sweetness and acidity of the slightly more luxurious rice adds to the best taste.  
I would rate Tokubetsu Junmai 4.0/5 and Tokubetsu Junmai Nigori 4.5/5.  
They cost 1,050 and 1,150 yen respectively, and each serving seemed comfortably larger than one go (180 ml). I'd call that good value.
