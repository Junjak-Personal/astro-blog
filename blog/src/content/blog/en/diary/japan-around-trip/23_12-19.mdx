### Arrival at Shin-Aomori Station  
  
I departed at 9 am and arrived at Shin-Aomori Station around 11:30.  
I felt it on the way, but it seems like it really snows a lot at the tip of Honshu.  
The streetscape, which appears to be lightly piled up for about 1m, is impressive.  
  
On the first floor of Shin-Aomori Station, there is a place called 何味館 (Shumikan), which is a collection of restaurants and shops selling specialty products, so I decided to take a look.  
As it is local sake and Aomori, there were a lot of apple-related souvenirs and specialties.  
There were also a few corners where you could sample gift snacks.  
From Matsumoto to Fukaura, I never saw any Koreans, but I occasionally heard Korean around Aomori. It's a nice feeling.  
  
Although it was lunch time, I ate quite a lot for breakfast in the morning, so I bought croquettes and mochi with the intention of snacking on them.  
The taste was ordinary. Does it seem like enough to simply fill your hunger?  
Nebuta, one of Aomori's symbols, was also on display at the station.

### Hirosaki Castle (first stamp)  
  
Hirosaki's first destination is Hirosaki Castle.  
I also purchased 100 reputation booklets and visited to get stamps.  
I took a bus to a nearby stop.  
It snowed quite a bit, and the park scenery beneath the snow-covered castle felt cozy.  
A guide to protecting plants everywhere from snow? It's impressive how many things are the same.  
  
After wandering around the park for a bit, I visited the Hirosaki Castle Information Center.  
There was a commemorative medal vending machine at the entrance, a place to sell Snow Miku-related goods, and event information.  
I was concerned about the event, so I scanned the QR and went in, and it seems that Hirosaki City is holding a winter tourism campaign, and that commemorative goods will be given when the campaign is completed.  
When you scan a QR with a scanner using an app called Mikunavi, a stamp is created. If you stamp a total of 5 stamps, the campaign is complete.  
I felt like I had received a quest while traveling, so I decided to carry out the quest.  
  
I was able to see quite a few sculptures inside the information center, probably because I was serious about the collaboration event with Snow Miku.  
Additionally, I purchased some Snow Miku-related goods.  
  
The keep of Hirosaki Castle is a three-story structure and is quite small.  
It was said to have been quite large in the past with a five-story structure, but its current appearance was restored in the early 1800s due to damage caused by a lightning strike.  
It is one of the famous castles with 12 existing castle towers.

### Fujita Memorial Garden (second stamp)  
  
After leaving Hirosaki Castle, I found the event location and planned my route to participate in the campaign in earnest.  
The first place to visit is the nearby Fujita Memorial Museum Western Hall. It is located southwest of the castle.  
  
The interior of Fujita Memorial Garden consists of two teahouses and a garden, and the garden is quite beautifully composed, so it seems worth a visit.  
Fortunately, the weather cleared up and the scenery was nice. Unfortunately, some areas were off-limits due to the accumulated snow.  
  
After touring the garden, I moved to the Fujita Western Museum. There was a stamp booth right at the entrance, so I registered my stamp right away.  
To take a break, I visited Taisho Roman Tea House. Luckily, I was able to get a seat by the window.  
The menu I ordered was a mixture of iced tea and apple juice and Yumeringo Apple Pie for a total of 1,100 yen.  
I later learned that this place is famous for its apple pie...  
The drinks were so-so, and although I personally don't like apple pie, this one was quite edible.  
  
After leaving Fujita Memorial Garden, I spotted a Starbucks on the way to my next stop. Its design was different from a typical branch, so I took a photo.  
I thought Hirosaki seemed like a city with a lot of personality.  
A city where you can feel the sincerity toward apples, so much so that you can even see apple sculptures in mailboxes.

### Tenmai (evening)  
  
For dinner, I found an izakaya restaurant called Tenmai and went there.  
The location was near the entrance to the hotel, so I was a bit skeptical while looking at the map as to whether this was the right place.  
It was run by a couple who appeared to be in their 50s or 60s, with the husband cooking and the wife serving the customers.  
On the menu, there was a nomi-kurabe set (5 types of local sake, 5 types of snacks), so I ordered it.  
  
The five types of local sake are from left to right: Tenshu, Hachitsuru, Uraotokoyama, Torai, and Inemurai.  
  
- **Tenshu Tokubetsu junmai**: Sake with a slightly dry taste and impressive umami  
- **Hachitsuru junmai ginjo unfiltered**: The bitter taste was a bit unique and I could feel the umami behind it, but it wasn't my taste.  
- **Uraotokoyama chokara junmai nama**: A fruity taste that puts the word chokara to shame. Sake, where you can feel the acidity and carbonation as much as it is Nama. It was pretty good  
- **Torai Chokara Tokubetsu Junmai**: A dry taste worthy of the karaguchi tag.  
- **Inemurai R5by Shikomi No. 8**: This was the driest and had a strong alcoholic feel.  
  
In addition, I also ordered Houhai (豊盃) Junmai Shiboritate Nama and Jokuu (如空) Hatsushibori Junmai Genshu.  
Houhai was very clean and seemed well suited to sashimi, while Jokuu was a decent, fairly unremarkable sake.  
  
Apart from the slightly expensive otoshi charge, prices were reasonable and the food was delicious. A good place to eat.  
It was a fun time because I was able to talk about my travels with the hostess, people’s lives, etc.  
I also learned how to say goodbye in the Aomori dialect: **hebana**.  
After eating a lot of snacks and drinks, it ended up being about 8,000 yen.

### After-dinner walk  
  
There was a park near Aspam that I had passed on the way earlier, so I went there to digest it.  
Even when viewed up close, it has a bizarre shape.  
I searched and looked for what it looked like during the day, and it looked fine during the day, but the green lighting created a mysterious atmosphere.  
  
The park is adjacent to the sea, so it was a good place to view the sea at night.  
There was a lot of snow piled up throughout the park (about 30cm?), so it was a bit inconvenient to move around.  
  
There is a Nebuta museum called Nebuta's House Warasse near the park, so I stopped by.  
The building was impressive because of its unique architecture.  
The Nebuta exhibition hall was closed as expected, but I looked around the Nebuta that was created in the open area for a bit and then returned to my accommodation.
