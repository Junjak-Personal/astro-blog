## Journal  
  
A late start today. We left the accommodation around 10 and headed to Cutlet Matsumura, where I'd booked brunch.  
Endoji shopping street was holding a little event. You wrote down your impressions of the street and hung them on an "Endoji constellation observation map," as in the photo below.  
When I visited Endoji shopping district when I studied abroad in Nagoya, there were events like this. It was nice to see that they seemed to have deep pride and affection for their shopping district.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112034720951.webp" alt="image-20250112034720951" />  
  
After passing through the small Konpira Shrine in the Endoji shopping district,  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112034926074.webp" alt="image-20250112034926074" />  
  
Visit the dango store at the end of the Endoji shopping street and buy and eat a dango (cheap at 90 yen each).  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112035332258.webp" alt="image-20250112035332258" />  
  
We arrived at our destination, the Dai Nagoya Building.  
Porsche was on display on the first floor.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112035434869.webp" alt="image-20250112035434869" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112035443760.webp" alt="image-20250112035443760" />  
  
Cutlet Matsumura is on the third floor of the Dai Nagoya Building.  
Reservations can be made through Tabelog, and if you visit without a reservation, there is a high chance of waiting, so we recommend making a reservation.  
https://maps.app.goo.gl/niVaLM2cgh6u2Qup6  
  
I tried this restaurant when I was traveling alone to Nagoya and it was so delicious that I recommended it to my companions.  
The menu items ordered were lunch hire and loin duo set for 3,300 yen, draft beer for 660 yen, and truffle potato croquette for 770 yen, for a total of 4,730 yen.  
  
The menu was served in a simple course format, and the first amuse was “fried Parmigiano Reggiano and sesame breadcrumbs.” The texture is crispy and delicious, making it great for whetting your appetite.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112035949073.webp" alt="image-20250112035949073" />  
  
I ordered a draft beer, followed by pickled ginger and cucumber.  
It has acidity, so it is suitable for cleaning the mouth before eating tonkatsu.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112040527252.webp" alt="image-20250112040527252" />  
  
Next came the extra truffle potato croquette I'd ordered.  
The batter and the degree of frying are excellent, with just the right amount of truffle flavor and a soft feel to the potatoes.  
The perfect example of crispy outside, soft inside. The croquette alone was filling enough to take the edge off my hunger.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112040839600.webp" alt="image-20250112040839600" />  
  
Then came the main course, the tenderloin and loin cutlet set. Miso soup was included too, although it isn't in the photo.  
Meat aged at low temperature is fried properly and has a great taste.  
The feeling of eating medium-rare well-grilled meat with crispy batter.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112041044266.webp" alt="image-20250112041044266" />  
  
Lastly, yogurt sherbet and hojicha  
It refreshes the mouth that was greasy from frying.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112041259490.webp" alt="image-20250112041259490" />  
  
This is a restaurant so popular and delicious that it was selected as one of Tabelog's 100 Best Stores 2024, so if you like tonkatsu, I think this is a place you should definitely stop by if you come to Nagoya.  
  
After lunch, take Meitetsu to Inuyama Station, our next destination.  
One of the group members was very interested in coffee, so we went to a famous drip coffee shop in Inuyama.  
  
The name is Coffee Botan. https://maps.app.goo.gl/htWkfyftSU4HYcuN7  
The store's signature idol, a cat, is cute and the interior is stylish.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112042038432.webp" alt="image-20250112042038432" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112041917564.webp" alt="image-20250112041917564" />  
  
It seems to be a store that coffee lovers will enjoy as there are many coffee beans to taste.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112041944151.webp" alt="image-20250112041944151" />  
  
Unusually, the cats kept at the store often go for walks outside.  
After wandering outside for a while, the cat somehow timed its return to customers entering and leaving the shop.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112042055698.webp" alt="image-20250112042055698" />  
  
For coffee, I chose Botan coffee beans, which were heavily roasted coffee beans, but I don't know much about it other than that it has a strong bitter taste.  
The group drank while admiring their drip skills...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112042151927.webp" alt="image-20250112042151927" />  
  
After spending some time at the cafe, we looked around Inuyama Castle Village in earnest.  
We stopped for takoyaki and an "adult" soft-serve ice cream topped with whisky.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112042401600.webp" alt="image-20250112042401600" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112042408153.webp" alt="image-20250112042408153" />  
  
In addition to the silkworm cocoon spinning experience, there was silkworm cocoon omikuji on the street, so I gave it a try.  
The price is 300 yen, and the result is the first great fortune of this trip.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112042417508.webp" alt="image-20250112042417508" />  
  
I didn't try it, but I admired an atmospheric shop selling grilled fish.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112042621363.webp" alt="image-20250112042621363" />  
  
While walking towards Inuyama Castle, visit Sankoinari Shrine in the middle.  
As expected from Inari Shrine, it is impressive to see a row of torii gates painted red.  
It's known for matchmaking, with love fortunes and heart-shaped ema prayer plaques.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112042731876.webp" alt="image-20250112042731876" />  
  
Sanko Waterfall.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112043125568.webp" alt="image-20250112043125568" />  
  
I don't think there was an omikuji like this when I came here before...  
I drew a love fortune too. It cost 500 yen, and I got medium luck.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112042849511.webp" alt="image-20250112042849511" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112042829587.webp" alt="image-20250112042829587" />  
  
I also stopped by Haritsuna Shrine near Sankoinari Shrine.  
The main deity is Owariharinane Muraji no Mikoto (尾治針名根連命). People visit to pray for long life and protection from misfortune.  
  
Here I tried an uomikuji, a fish-shaped fortune.  
Strangely, I used something like a fishing rod to hook a fish like the one in the picture and pulled it out, but I had a hard time because it didn't get caught on the hook very well.  
It cost 300 yen. I got good luck and tied the fortune up at the shrine.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112043156873.webp" alt="image-20250112043156873" />  
  
Walk a little further from the shrine and arrive at Inuyama Castle. Admission fee is 550 yen.  
There was a long line to get in, and it seemed like there were more people than when I came 5 years ago.  
Inuyama Castle is one of the five most famous castles in Japan and is famous for having its castle tower designated as a national treasure. It is one of the few castles that still retains its original appearance rather than having its castle tower restored.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112044001341.webp" alt="image-20250112044001341" />  
  
One of the attractions is that you can see the panoramic view of Inuyama city from the observatory at the top of the castle tower.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112044536619.webp" alt="image-20250112044536619" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112044555950.webp" alt="image-20250112044555950" />  
  
I came down from the castle and looked around the castle village a little more before returning to Inuyama Station.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112044630167.webp" alt="image-20250112044630167" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112044714829.webp" alt="image-20250112044714829" />

Unagi marinated in soy sauce that is just delicious. The price is 500 yen.

I took the Meitetsu and got off at Meitetsu Jingumae Station and moved to Horaiken.  
Since Horaiken does not allow advance reservations and has a waiting system upon arrival, I decided to wait and take a look at Atsuta Shrine.  
  
When I got off at Jingumae Station, there was a new shopping street that didn't exist 5 years ago.  
It was called Atsuta Nagaya and looked newly opened.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112045408870.webp" alt="image-20250112045408870" />  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112045431247.webp" alt="image-20250112045431247" />  
  
The omiyage shop here sells various specialties, jizake, etc.  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112045627446.webp" alt="image-20250112045627446" />  
  
Purchase a Nagoya Ebisenbei here. It was about 700 yen for 6 pieces.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112045700910.webp" alt="image-20250112045700910" />  
  
Arrived at Horaiken, waited and went to see Atsuta Shrine.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112045528590.webp" alt="image-20250112045528590" />  
  
Because it was night, entry to the main shrine was not possible, and the surrounding area was dark and not suitable for viewing.  
After looking around for a bit, I left and waited in Horaiken's waiting room.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250112050008824.webp" alt="image-20250112050008824" />  
  
I entered the store about 2 hours after waiting.  
https://maps.app.goo.gl/zDvFwftKQSCTYBgG9  
  
Horaiken's Hitsumabushi was worth the long wait.  
The taste of the eel itself was not much different from the hitsumabushi restaurant called Maruya in Nagoya Station.  
The biggest difference is the excellent service that makes me feel like I am being treated well.  
Of course, the taste of hitsumabushi was excellent.  
Although there are four ways to enjoy hitsumabushi,  
Personally, I like to eat ochazuke, so I ate it mostly as ochazuke.  
  
For my last dessert, I ordered pudding, and it was a delicacy as it had a sweetness and softness that wasn't overpowering.  
Apparently, the pudding comes from a nearby wagashi shop. If you visit Horaiken's Jingu branch, I highly recommend it for dessert.

After dinner, we decided to relax at a super sento bathhouse.  
Urban Quar Spa & Living, near Higashibetsuin Station. https://maps.app.goo.gl/pbBHChfNjNVMiEX98  
I didn't take any pictures, but this is a super sento that many locals visit in Nagoya.  
Perhaps because it was Saturday, there were quite a lot of people.  
Enter by paying 1,300 yen for the bath + rock bath set.  
After enjoying the full course of the regular bath, open-air bath, sauna, and rock bath, I returned to the accommodation.
