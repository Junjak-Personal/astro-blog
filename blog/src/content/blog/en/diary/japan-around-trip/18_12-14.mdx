It is one of the first parks created after the Meiji period and is quite well developed.

A panel introducing the relationship between the Sengoku military commanders, Hakusan Castle, and the shrine.  
Hakusan Castle, the site of the Uesugi clan civil war during the Warring States Period, was built in the area around the current Hakusan Shrine and park.  
It is said that during the Edo period, commercial activities were permitted only in this area, making it a commercial center.

Next, I visited Exhibition Hall 2, but photography/video shooting was not allowed here.  
In the first space, a special video adapting the live house performance scene from episode 8 of the animation and the school festival performance scene from episode 12 was played.

Very satisfied.

The permanent exhibition alone might not justify the trip, but it's a nice place to drop into if you have time after a special exhibition.

### Ponshukan and dinner  
  
I moved back to Niigata Station to complete the last schedule of the day, Ponshukan.  
There was an unidentifiable JR character near the JR ticket gate.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.19.17.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.19.17" />  
  
Ponshukan is located in the southwest direction inside the station, but it was a bit difficult to get there by just looking at the map inside the station, as there were almost no directions.  
It was a tourist attraction, but there seemed to be more locals than tourists.  
There were only a handful of foreigners.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.20.42.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.20.42" />

Most tasting tables charge 1 coin per glass, and there are some that are a bit more expensive, costing 2 coins.  
Does the price feel reasonable?  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.21.01.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.21.01" />

The sheer scale of the place left me overwhelmed.  
I think it's a great place for people who like sake.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.20.28.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.20.28" />

It's a bit of a waste of money to try this, so skip it.  
Full of highly rated sake labels such as Zaku, Kamikawa Josetsu, Abe, and Dassai.

Sake tasting in earnest.  
There was detailed information about the alcohol at each tasting station, making it easy to choose.  
  
What I liked was numbers 109, 34, and 26, which were all drinks priced at 2 coins.  
No. 109 is sake stored in whiskey-aged oak barrels, and the whiskey aroma and woody feeling exuding from the sake was attractive.  
Although the alcohol content is high, it is offset by the aroma and sweetness.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.25.29.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.25.29" />  
  
Next is the 34 Kamonishiki Niigata Edition.  
As described on the right, there were subtle melon and other fruit aromas. The sweetness was restrained and clean, making it seem like a good sake to drink with a meal.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.25.45.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.25.45" />

It also felt neat and clean, fitting for Karaguchido 3.

Here, I received 5 stamps (stamped by a staff member) and checked the Sakagura I ate.  
I don't know how many years it will take, but my goal is to fill all the dojos.

Since I drank a lot at Ponshukan, I went to eat hangover ramen.  
There is a restaurant corner at Niigata Station, and there are a lot of ramen restaurants there.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.23.08.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.23.08" />  
  
The Darumaya Niigata base I chose from one of the stores there.  
The Google rating wasn't very good, but I wanted to eat tonkotsu ramen, so I decided to go here.  
What I ordered was chashu tonkotsu ramen with minced garlic, carami sauce, seaweed toppings, and fried rice for a total of 1,630 yen.  
It's cheap compared to what I ordered.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.22.38.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-02-26_23.22.38" />  
  
The tonkotsu was quite tasty and the fried rice was a little dry, but it was still delicious.  
The restaurant currently has a Google rating of around 3, but judging by the food alone, I think it deserves something in the high threes.  
I looked up some reviews and found that many people gave it a low rating because the quality was inconsistent.
