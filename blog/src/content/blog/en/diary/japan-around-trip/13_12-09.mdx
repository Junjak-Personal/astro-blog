## Journal  
  
Since breakfast was included at the ryokan, I woke up at 7:30 in the morning and had breakfast.  
The menu is Hobamiso steak and typical Japanese home cooking. There was grilled mackerel, egg rolls, vegetables, and miso soup.  
The Hoba Miso Steak is grilled with locally made soybean paste on Hoba leaves as shown in the photo, and I was surprised because it was my first time seeing it.  
The taste is a little sweeter than regular miso and has a certain amount of saltiness, so it is very suitable as a side dish for rice.  
The other dishes were also delicious, so I ate them all without leaving any.

<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250114030941344.webp" alt="image-20250114030941344" />  
  
After eating, I headed to the Green Hotel where I went yesterday to take a morning hot spring bath.  
Unfortunately, day-use bathing didn't start until 11 a.m. It was a wasted trip, and I returned to the ryokan.  
Still, the weather was clear on the way back, so it was fun to see the scenery.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250114031627444.webp" alt="image-20250114031627444" />  
  
After getting ready to go out from the hotel again, I decided to take a look around the Miyagawa morning market.  
Tourists and locals were mixed together, forming a procession.  
There aren't too many people, just the right amount. It seems to be a fairly lively market.

I ordered midarashi dango and a croissant at the market.  
The midarashi dango was around 300 yen, which was a bit disappointing considering the price, but the croissant was crispy and delicious.

After briefly looking around Miyagawa Market, we moved to the bus stop to go to Shirakawa-go.  
I lost my ticket on the way and had to pay 5,600 yen again to purchase it...  
There are reserved seats and regular seats, but you had to wait quite a long time for the regular seats.  
I aimed to catch the bus at 10 a.m., but the line in front was already large, so I was able to board the bus at about 10:40.  
I thought the schedule would be completely different, but fortunately, there were a lot of buses, so it was a little later than planned, but there was no major disruption to the plan.  
  
It takes about an hour to arrive at Shirakawa-go by bus.  
I was moved by the snowy scenery I had only seen on YouTube or in photos unfolding before my eyes.  
Clear skies would have made it even better, but I was just grateful to see the snow.

We also visited the Wada Residence along the way, though I didn't take any proper photos.  
I thought that Japanese houses made of gassho-zukuri would have taken a lot of measures to protect against cold.  
Except for the roof, there was no significant difference from a typical Japanese house. So it was very cold inside the house...  
The biggest difference was the roof, so I looked at the roof closely.

After looking around the town for a bit, we went to Tanakaya for lunch.  
 https://maps.app.goo.gl/8QPnwG3UyCbVtFnL7  
This is a very popular store, so there was a long queue after we entered.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250114033827120.webp" alt="image-20250114033827120" />  
  
I ordered the Hida beef hoba miso set, a flask of hot local sake, and kata tofu as a drinking snack.  
Before the main meal arrived, I started with the hot sake and tofu.  
As its name suggests, the firm tofu was harder than the usual tofu I eat in Korea. The hot sake was ordinary enough.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250114033947600.webp" alt="image-20250114033947600" />  
  
Then the main meal arrived.  
Hida beef, hoba miso, kata tofu, soba (can also be served cold), vegetables, and grilled saury, a fairly filling meal for 1,750 yen.  
Everything was delicious. I'd happily call this a good restaurant.  
Why did the Hida beef + Hobamiso I ate here taste more delicious than the Hida beef I ate yesterday...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250114033753272.webp" alt="image-20250114033753272" />  
  
After lunch, I visited a cafe.  
The name is Hanamizuki.  
It was an attractive cafe with a view of Shirakawa-go from the wide window, but unfortunately there were a lot of people so I had to sit against the wall.  
I ordered warm citron tea, and they also gave me some cookies. The taste was no different from ordinary citron tea sold on the market.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250114034343978.webp" alt="image-20250114034343978" />

The store in the souvenir shop was selling churros shaped like the letters “Higurashi” so I bought one.  
Perhaps they shaped each letter by hand with the churro machine; it took about 15 minutes to come out.  
At 1,100 yen, it was expensive and tasted like an ordinary churro. A souvenir, after all.

After touring Shirakawa-go, when returning to Takayama, I stood in line about 40 minutes before the bus departure time. (If you miss the bus, you won't be able to make it to the ryokan dinner time.)  
It took about 50 minutes to return to Takayama, about 10 minutes earlier than when we came.  
  
  
  
After arriving at the hotel, I washed up and rested, and had dinner at the ryokan at 6:30 PM.  
The price is 3,300 yen per person for one dinner.  
The menu was generous with various pickles, vegetables, sashimi, tempura, mini Hida beef sukiyaki, etc. as follows.  
The sashimi was well aged and easy to eat, and the tempura was also delicious because the skin was crispy.  
Sukiyaki is a taste that needs no words.  
  
For pairing, the Miyamagiku Namazake from Funasaka Brewery, which I sampled yesterday, and the Kokuryu Junmai Daiginjo, which I had stocked up on.  
The Kokuryu junmai daiginjo paired especially well with the sashimi.  
Miyamagiku Namazake was dry and went well with the food.  
  
I felt it in the morning as well, but this ryokan is good at cooking, so all the food was delicious.  
If you stay at a Sumiyoshi ryokan, it would be a good idea to eat breakfast and dinner.  
The price is not expensive either.

After dinner, we rested a bit and visited a place called “Bar Mellow,” which was one of the bars we had been eyeing.  
  
Unusually, a man who did hypnotism and magic was performing at the bar.  
Out of curiosity and at the bartender's suggestion, I tried being hypnotized. The performer didn't seem very skilled, though... it didn't work on me.  
I watched his magic too, which didn't seem particularly good either. There went 1,000 yen for the show.  
  
The cocktail menu was generally simple, mixing liqueur + ginger ale or carbonated water.  
I ordered two drinks, but it didn't make much of an impact and I couldn't remember the name of the menu.  
The bartender was also preoccupied with chatting to the woman next to me, which didn't leave a good impression...  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250114035714735.webp" alt="image-20250114035714735" />  
  
After leaving the bar, I wandered around looking for another place to drink.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250114035730258.webp" alt="image-20250114035730258" />  
  
In the end, I returned to Bar Thumbs Up.  
I visited an hour before closing, so luckily there were no other customers.  
I finished the day here with a simple craft beer called Osaru IPA and sausage snacks.  
The owner here really is lovely.
