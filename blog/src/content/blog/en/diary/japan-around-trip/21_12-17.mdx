#### Marukawa - Lunch  
  
After a brief tour of the Aoyagi residence for about 40 minutes, I visited Marukawa Udon Restaurant for lunch.  
The purpose was to try Kiritanpo Nabe, one of Akita's specialties.  
Compared to other nearby restaurants, there weren't many reviews based on Google Maps, but the rating was quite high at 4.5 points, so I visited.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-03-29_03.34.07@2x.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-03-29_03.34.07@2x" />  
  
It was lunch time, but there were no customers inside except me.  
The menu I ordered was Kiritanpo Nabe, Kamojiru Udon, and Sapporo Lager for a total of 3,100 yen.  
  
Kiritanpo nabe is so warm and savory in soy sauce-based broth that it feels like your frozen body is melting.  
Add a glass of beer to it and it's paradise.  
Kiritanpo felt like bamboo rice, but it was a bit like rice cake.  
It felt fresh as I had never tried it before, and of course the taste was great.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-03-29_03.38.06@2x.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-03-29_03.38.06@2x" />  
  
Kamojiru Udon appeared next.  
The udon is called Inaniwa, an Akita specialty, and has a slightly kalguksu-like feel.  
The noodles were cold and springy, so I dipped them in kamojiru and it was quite good.  
Kamojiru is also a soy sauce-based dish for tsukemen, so it had a slightly saltier taste compared to Kiritanpo Nabe.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-03-29_03.40.06.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-03-29_03.40.06" />  
  
Next beer.  
It can also be said to be a restorative medicine that restores the body tired from travel.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/CleanShot_2025-03-29_03.42.16@2x.webp" alt="CleanShot_2025-03-29_03.42.16@2x" />

### Furofushi hot springs  
  
It takes about 2 hours and 40 minutes by train from Akita Station to Henashi Station, near Furofushi Onsen.  
When I arrived near Henassi Station, I wondered if I had to walk for about 30 minutes to the accommodation, but fortunately the accommodation sent a shuttle bus.  
I later learned that a pickup shuttle could be requested from nearby stations, and that scheduled shuttle buses also ran to Shin-Aomori Station.  
  
After many twists and turns, I arrived at the hotel around 6 PM.  
The downside is the poor transport access, as the inn is right at the far end of Honshu.  
  
A fun painting introducing the Aomori dialect hanging in the hallway leading to the room.  
I was thankful that it was also written in Korean.

#### Furofushi hot springs  
  
After dinner, I decided to enjoy the hot springs, which is the main attraction of this hotel.  
The hot springs available to hotel guests are the beach, main building, and new building. Day bathers can only use the beach and main building hot springs.  
According to the information, the famous beach open-air hot springs are closed after sunset for safety reasons, so I decided to visit tomorrow morning and use the new building hot springs.  
  
The new building hot spring consists of one dry sauna, two internal baths (hot spring, regular cold bath), and one open-air hot spring.  
The open-air hot spring was very windy (the temperature was -1 degrees Celsius, but it felt like -10 degrees Celsius) and it was quite cold, so it was difficult to stay there for a long time.  
The feeling of looking out at the dark but rippling night sea from a snowy open-air bath is a valuable experience you won't find anywhere else.  
I took the time to stare blankly at the waves and reflect on what happened today.  
  
  
After taking a hot spring bath, I went to sleep looking forward to seeing this scenery with my own eyes the next morning.
