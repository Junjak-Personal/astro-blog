## Journal  
  
### Departure  
  
It has snowed a lot since yesterday, and it snowed a lot again today, so I was worried that my flight might be canceled or significantly delayed.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250102233525984.webp" alt="image-20250102233525984" />  
  
As shown in the photo above, most flights were delayed and some were canceled.  
Fortunately, my flight from Incheon to Kitakyushu was delayed by about an hour. A friend who was traveling to Japan on the same day had an Air Seoul flight, but it was canceled, so he ended up canceling his trip as well.  
  
After a flight of about two hours, I arrived in Kitakyushu to clear skies and rather strong winds.  
From Kitakyushu Airport, I took a bus straight to Kokura Station, exchanged my voucher for a 21-day JR Rail Pass at the JR Travel Center, and immediately boarded a Shinkansen to start the first day's itinerary.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250102234200707.webp" alt="image-20250102234200707" />  
  
The first day's schedule was Kitakyushu Kokura Station → Fukuyama → Tomonoura → Okayama, as shown in the Google timeline below.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250102234209852.webp" alt="image-20250102234209852" />  
  
The Shinkansen from Kokura to Fukuyama took about one hour and 40 minutes. I rode Sakura No. 550, and it really was fast... expensive, though.  
  
### Fukuyama and Tomonoura  
  
When I arrived at Fukuyama Station, I was surprised to see Fukuyama Castle as shown in the photo below right from the station platform.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250102234305316.webp" alt="image-20250102234305316" />  
  
I left my luggage in a coin locker and went into the station to find lunch. Exploring restaurants would have been nice, but with the bus to Tomonoura in mind, I kept it simple and visited a ramen shop inside the station called Ikkokusakigakedo.  
  
 https://maps.app.goo.gl/8VknsaVpufQNQPqQA  
  
I ordered the kotteri ramen and karaage lunch special for 1,200 yen. It was a fairly ordinary bowl of tonkotsu ramen with fairly ordinary fried chicken.  
Neither especially good nor bad... I was happy to get a simple, inexpensive meal.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250102234310928.webp" alt="image-20250102234310928" />  
  
After lunch and the bus ride, I arrived in Tomonoura at about 2:30 p.m.  
The coastal scenery is beautiful.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250102234324667.webp" alt="image-20250102234324667" />  
  
Tomonoura used to be a thriving port town, but is now a quiet and secluded place with traces of the past.  
There are quite a few historical sites connected to Sakamoto Ryoma. Hayao Miyazaki is also said to have loved the town. It felt like a lovely place to unwind, with very few tourists around...  
  
One of Tomonoura's specialties is homeishu, a medicinal liqueur. There are quite a few breweries around town.  
One of them was Irie Homeishu. I saw homeishu-flavored soft-serve ice cream for sale there and tried one.  
It was about 450 yen, if I remember correctly. It tasted like ice cream flavored with red ginseng candy? I can't really recommend it.  
I also visited another Irie Homeishu shop, sampled the liqueur, and bought a bottle as a souvenir.  
The homeishu itself tasted like alcohol flavored with red ginseng candy?  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250102234318210.webp" alt="image-20250102234318210" />  
  
This is Tomonoura's signature view.

<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250102234637121.webp" alt="image-20250102234637121" />

I deliberately spent more time because I wanted to see the sunset scenery.  
A quiet little fishing village with lovely scenery. It felt genuinely relaxing.  
  
Leaving the main street behind, I wandered around a little. My first stop was the Irohamaru Exhibition Hall.  
Admission was around 200 yen, I think. The reviews weren't particularly good, but inside were exhibits and explanations about the Irohamaru, the ship Sakamoto Ryoma used for trade before it sank.  
As usual, museums and exhibition halls were a bit challenging for me, so I had a quick look and moved on.  
  
Next was Nunakuma Shrine.  
Apparently, it dates back to the Heian period and enshrines deities including Susanoo. It didn't look particularly well maintained, but it was quite a large shrine.

I wanted to stop at a cafe along the way. By chance, I spotted one with a lovely interior and decided to go in.  
It was called Soup and Onigiri Crank.  
  
https://maps.app.goo.gl/XfWYYsMkEV2dNfSP9

I'd been curious about craft cola since hearing it mentioned on a Japanese radio show I often listen to. Lucky me.  
Matcha tiramisu for dessert.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250103000231849.webp" alt="image-20250103000231849" />

I imagine the flavors vary by region, so I'd like to try craft cola from other parts of Japan too.  
The matcha tiramisu was good because it was moderately sweet and moderately bitter.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250103000414616.webp" alt="image-20250103000414616" />

Next, I headed to Fukuzenji Temple.  
The road was quiet and nice, so I took a picture.

### Okayama  
  
The Shinkansen from Fukuyama to Okayama took about 16 minutes. (The JR Rail Pass is the best.)  
First, I checked in to the accommodation, rested a bit, and then went out for dinner.  
  
Every promising place I found on Google Maps seemed packed with office workers having group dinners. Was there something special about today? After striking out at about three places, I happened across an izakaya called Dandantei and went in.  
  
https://maps.app.goo.gl/BmCL9FGeA3fd6yPs6  
  
It was run by an elderly couple, and the owner was wonderfully cheerful when taking my order.  
At a Japanese izakaya, beer comes first. The draft was Asahi, just what I needed after a tiring day of travel.  
The otoshi appetizer was something like steamed sea snail, and it was quite good.  
It was a little chilly, so I ordered hot sake too. On a cold day, hot sake really is the best.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250103001715467.webp" alt="image-20250103001715467" />  
  
There was assorted sashimi on the menu, so I ordered it.  
There are 4 types: Sawara (11 o'clock), Tai (7 o'clock), Shima Aji (1 o'clock), and Shimesaba (5 o'clock).  
All were well aged, fresh and delicious.  
Sashimi goes better with chilled sake than hot sake, so I asked the owner for recommendations and ordered two local Okayama varieties.  
It wasn't particularly delicious, so I didn't record it... One was moderately sweet, and the other was dry.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250103001728315.webp" alt="image-20250103001728315" />  
  
I also ordered grilled eihire, dried ray fins, one of the snacks I'd heard about on the radio.  
I'd never seen it in Korea, but it seems common in Japanese izakayas. It fills a similar role to dried pollock as a drinking snack.  
It's quite delicious when dipped in mayonnaise.  
I added pork kakuni and shijimi clam miso soup.  
The braised pork was tender and delicious. The clam miso soup was wonderfully soothing, almost like a hangover cure. A perfect way to finish the meal.  
  
<ImageLoader src="/files/blog/diary/japan-around-trip/assets/image-20250103001745204.webp" alt="image-20250103001745204" />  
  
The total, including the otoshi, came to 7,000 yen.  
  
The owner kindly struck up a conversation, and we talked about all sorts of things. When I explained that I was traveling around Japan, we started with travel and recent relations between Korea and Japan.  
It could have been a sensitive topic, but he was friendly toward Korea and had Zainichi friends. We could discuss history without things feeling uncomfortable, which I appreciated.  
He seemed fairly elderly but was also up to date on recent anime, so we talked quite a bit about Attack on Titan and Demon Slayer.  
Traveling alone doesn't always leave many chances to talk to people. His kindness gave me a lovely, memorable evening.  
We agreed that next time I came to Okayama, I'd bring some good Korean makgeolli for us to drink together. Haha.  
I also received a business card~  
  
The food is quite delicious and the atmosphere is nice, so I recommend visiting if you get the chance.
