### Matsushima coast  
  
Today I was going to Matsushima Kaigan, one of Japan's three celebrated scenic views.  
The weather was clear and nice until I arrived on the train.  
  
The subway station was located slightly higher than the coast, so it had a view of the coastal town of Matsushima.  
  
I hadn't had breakfast yet, so I went into a nearby restaurant for brunch around eleven.  
The weather, which had been clear, suddenly started snowing and I became a little anxious.  
The restaurant I went into was Gyutan Sumiyaki Rikyu, and the menu I ordered was the Gyutan Yaki & Kaisendon Set for 3,278 yen.  
The Kaisendon was delicious as usual, and the gyutan yaki seemed to be the main dish, as the name of the restaurant suggests.  
As shown in the photo, it was cooked fairly well to medium rare, so it was soft and had a unique texture, so it was quite delicious.  
At the manager's recommendation, we also tried grilled oysters for 400 yen, splitting the cost with my friend. Those were a little underwhelming.  
It was a bit expensive, but it wasn't a bad meal.

### Kamaboko Experience & Curry Bread  
  
After having breakfast, I looked around a bit and saw that there was a store called Matsukama that grilled their own kamaboko, so I went there out of curiosity.  
Kamaboko was a little small at around 300 yen, but considering the price of tourist attractions, it was a reasonable price, and it wasn't bad since you could also have such an amazing experience.  
I tried grilling it until the Maillard browning reaction came out well, but it tasted like an ordinary kamaboko.  
Well, there was no way it had a special taste, but I think I was able to give it a passing grade because it had romance.  
  
Next, I bought and ate gyutan curry bread, and although I didn't expect it, it was quite delicious.  
It was snowy and windy on the way, so I went to a nearby cafe called Sakusaku, had a cup of hot coffee, and took a break from the snowy wind.

### Fukaura  
  
When the snowstorm subsided, I walked to Fukaura, where I could get a good view of the Matsushima coast.  
The entrance fee is quite affordable, around 200 yen.  
It was clear until I entered the bridge, but after that the snow and wind became more severe and the scenery became quite disappointing.  
A disaster in which the various islands of Matsushima seen from a distance were blurred due to the snowstorm, and the photos were taken a little strangely due to the snowfall.  
By the time I came out, it was as clear as a ghost again.  
In Tohoku, I also felt it in Niigata and Akita, but the weather seems to fluctuate too much.

### Kodaiji & Rest House  
  
After touring Fukaura, I visited Kodaiji. There was a temple on a small island connected by a bridge, and there was a small Hachiman Shrine, perhaps due to the influence of the fusion of Shinto and Buddhism.  
As you'd expect at a shrine, there was also an omikuji stand. I couldn't resist and paid 100 yen to draw one.  
I can't even remember if the result was a horse road, a road, or a small road.  
  
At Kodaiji, as seen in the photo, the snow blew and I went to the Matsushima Coast Rest House (cum-tourist information center) to rest.  
Here again, I saw Onsen Musume and bought 1,800 yen worth of goods.  
Her name is Matsushima Nazuki. I was pleased to see my collection gradually growing.

### Japanese Restaurant e - First Half  
  
Afterwards, my friend said he wasn't feeling well because he had been exposed to a lot of snow, so I rested at the hotel for about 2 hours after returning.  
After resting, it was almost dinner time and I visited Japanese restaurant e.  
The store had a luxurious feel right from the entrance, and as I sat at the counter, they served me water in a pretty glass.  
I took a picture because I liked the glass.  
  
Next, I ordered Gunma Izumi Yamahai-moto Junmai served hot to warm myself up.  
I'd originally wanted Tamazakura's Shingari, but it was sold out. Instead, there were just two servings left of this sake, so my friend and I had one each.  
Its rustic rice notes spread pleasantly, and I thought it was a good sake to enjoy warm.  
  
The first course was Sakizuke, a seasoned dish with salmon roe and mushrooms.  
The acidity stood out and the texture of the salmon roe and mushrooms was impressive. A dish suitable as an appetizer.  
  
The second course was Kumizakana (組肴), with ankimo, chawanmushi, etc. plated in a Japanese style.  
Everything was delicious, but the cheese and fried shrimp at the 6 o'clock position were especially impressive. The batter was thin and just right, so the texture was good.  
  
The third course is suimono (吸いwater), a soup containing shirako, tamagotofu, and yuzu.  
The flavor wasn't particularly memorable, but it worked well as a palate cleanser between courses.  
  
I told the chef that, as a foreign visitor, I found the ingredients hard to follow even when the dishes were explained, and asked if there was anything written down. He kindly wrote out the course order and ingredients on a Post-it note for me.  
They also took care of all the missing people, which I felt was really kind.

### Restaurant e - main course  
  
The fourth course is Otsukuri (御造り), which includes hirame and maguro sashimi from Miyagi Prefecture.  
The tuna was otoro—extremely rich, fatty, and tender—while the flounder was pleasantly chewy and fresh.  
  
The sake pairing was the legendary Juyondai.  
I've been to many izakayas in Japan, but this is the first time I've seen Juyondai on the menu, so I ordered it right away because I thought I had to order it. The price is 5,000 yen for one go, and the name is Juyondai Extra Hakutsuru Nishiki.  
Befitting Juyondai's reputation for reaching the pinnacle of hojun umakuchi sake, it was sweet, with the umami of rice, and, like junmai daiginjo, clean. Namazake had just the right amount of carbonation, making it the most delicious sake I've ever tasted.  
It was also a superb match for the sashimi. If you see Juyondai on a restaurant's menu, I strongly recommend trying it. (A 720 ml bottle costs around 27,000 yen on Rakuten, so this price is actually quite reasonable...)  
  
The fifth course is Takiawase (炊き合わせ), a dish with puffer fish, uni, and caviar placed in a bowl made of citron peel.  
Personally, this was the most disappointing course. Perhaps my palate isn't that sophisticated, but I didn't particularly enjoy the texture or flavor of the caviar. The pufferfish and uni were delicious, though.  
  
The sixth course is Nanbanzuke (南蛮漬け), fried anago and potato salad.  
As I felt at Kumizakana earlier, this restaurant is very good at fried food. The texture and taste when it reached the anago through the thin but crispy batter was fantastic. It goes well with the garnish of potato salad, so I personally found it to be the most delicious dish of the day.  
  
The seventh course was shiizakana (強肴): Sendai beef roast beef with shungiku and daikon.  
The beef was cooked just right and paired reasonably well with the garland chrysanthemum and radish. A straightforward, tasty beef dish.  
  
The eighth course was oshokuji (お食事): kamameshi rice cooked with kinmedai, shiso, yuzu, negi, and other ingredients.  
The rice course arrived while I still had some room left, which was welcome. The kinmedai was tender and delicious, as you'd expect of a premium fish. The shiso and yuzu gave the rice a slight acidity that felt unfamiliar at first, but it was so good I asked for seconds.  
I paired the kamameshi with Kenkonichi Junmaishu Kame no O Namazake.  
With its 70% rice-polishing ratio, some rustic rice notes came through. But the gentle fizz of the unpasteurized sake, served cold, kept them from becoming distracting, and it paired well with the rice.

## Finishing & Accommodation  
  
The final course is Mizukashi (水菓子), an orange liqueur jelly + strawberry dessert and hoshigaki (dried persimmon) wrapped in nukicha and fried.  
It wasn't especially delicious, but it was a reasonable dessert to end the course.  
  
Overall, both the service and the food were excellent. It's a restaurant I'd like to visit again.  
I also liked the appropriate distance from the master, who kindly accepted me when I asked for an explanation or talked to him.  
Also, as a service, they wrapped the leftover kamemeshi into rice balls, so you could have a meal for tomorrow.  
  
I think this dinner was worth the reservation made two months ago.  
  
After eating, I arrived at the accommodation and looked around the accommodation that I hadn't seen properly yet.  
Around ten or eleven at night, they offered complimentary yonaki soba, which I tried too. (I'd skipped it yesterday because I was full.)  
End the day after enjoying the hot springs.  
Tomorrow's schedule—Sendai -> Ueno -> Narita Airport—was quite demanding, and there wasn't much time before my flight. I needed an early start, so I went to bed early.
